# atrium.show

Atrium's website. Its job is to show the art: the wallpapers are the product and the site is their gallery. The words, the download button and the credits all step aside for them. It's a static site on Cloudflare Pages, served from `site/public/`. The app is described in `../CLAUDE.md`, and each wallpaper in `../docs/`.

`../../CLAUDE.md` belongs to dtanquary.com. Its design system (Dark Liquid Glass, Inter, #6eb1ff) doesn't apply here; only its Cloudflare account rule does. That file sits outside this repo, which is public, and it's the only place the account ID is written down. Never put the account ID, an API token, or a `wrangler.toml` with `account_id` in this repo.

**The design is `design.md`**, the Claude Design handoff: hard rules, the verbatim copy, colour and type tokens, and nine directions. Its layout values came from frames drawn in HTML; the lab pages carry them now. Where this file and `design.md` differ, `design.md` wins on look and copy, and this file on how the site is built.

## Principles, in order
1. **The art is the page.** Atrium's own wallpapers fill the screen, moving (at least 80% of the viewport). The site draws no decoration of its own: no gradient blobs, glows, glass, noise, 3D, stock photos or AI-generated images. The exceptions, which Dave asked for: Liquid Glass on the pane of cards, and a pretend macOS menu bar and Dock. The only pictures are Atrium's own renders, its Settings window and its icon. The page is near black (`#0A0A0B`), and its one accent comes from the work on screen.
2. **Calm, like the app.** Dave's direction in `../docs/README.md` applies here too: slow, ambient, soft and dim. Crossfades of a second or more, never a hard cut, even on first paint. No bounce, spring, parallax or scroll-jacking; the page scrolls like a page.
3. **Modern by being native.** `@property`, `color-mix()` in OKLCH, `animation-timeline: view()`, `clip-path`, variable fonts, AV1. Where a feature is missing, the page falls back to a still one that is just as beautiful.
4. **Honest.** Only the copy in `design.md`. No invented testimonials, download counts, press quotes or features.

## What's built
The site is direction 2b, One Endless Canvas, from `design.md`.
- `public/index.html`: one fixed canvas: six works with labels (title, type, line), then the download screen, still on Fish Tank. Each section is one screen tall, with `data-work` and its words; consecutive sections with the same work hold it still, and only the words travel, at the page's own speed. Every label sits in the same place, 130px down at the left margin (56px, 24px on phones), and fades by its section's progress (`view-timeline`): in as it rises into place, out just after, before it reaches the site's bar. Where the art behind a label is bright, its words are lifted (lighter, same hue), never moved. On the download screen, "Over 20 wallpapers included" opens a Liquid Glass pane (a native `<dialog>`) with a card for every wallpaper, and each card opens a second glass dialog over it playing that wallpaper's preview loop (Esc or Close goes back to the pane). A pretend macOS menu bar (Finder's menus, Atrium's status icon, a live clock) and a glass Dock show only on the laptop's screen: `laptop.css` shows them and fades them with the zoom, so they're gone while a wallpaper fills the window, and if the laptop is reverted there are none. Our own icons and no Apple logo, so none of Apple's artwork or marks; decorative and `aria-hidden`. None of this glass or desktop is in `design.md`: Dave asked for it (2026-10-01).
- `public/site.js`: the stage (one looping video per work, stacked full screen), the scroll-driven dissolve, the accent, the glass pane and its previews (a card's slug comes from its image's name), the pretend clock and calendar, and pausing: with the tab, and from the Pause button in the top bar (WCAG 2.2.2).
- `public/laptop.js` and `laptop.css`: the opening, on every screen. In the dark a closed laptop (our own drawing, no logo: a lid with bezel, notch and plain back, a deck with keyboard and trackpad, a front edge, all in CSS 3D) opens its lid as the page loads, and the first screen of scrolling scales its screen up to fill the window. The last screen pulls back out to the laptop, still showing Fish Tank, with the download words in the dark below it (tagline, Download and the pane's button on the left, the feature lines on the right). The base's back edge is exactly as wide as the lid, so its corners meet the lid's squared bottom corners, and perspective widens it toward you into a trapezoid; don't inset it to make the front flush (Dave: the screen then looks bigger than the laptop). The script builds the laptop around the page's own stage, menu bar and Dock and sets `html.laptop`, which every rule in `laptop.css` hangs off, so deleting the two lines in `index.html` that load them (or reverting its one commit) turns it off. On portrait screens (most phones) the laptop's screen is landscape, as tall as the window, and plays its own small landscape loops (`*-laptop.*`, 960 wide); as it zooms it slides so the portrait crop's focal point (mirrored from `media.sh` in `laptop.js`) meets the window's centre, so when it fills the window it shows exactly the region, at the scale, of the full-window wallpaper behind it. That fades in over the last fifth of the zoom (`--canvas`), with its loop set to the laptop loop's moment first; the end does the reverse. A new first or last work needs its focal point in that list. Reduce Motion, and arriving partway down the page, start it open. The notch is its own surface on the lid, in front of the screen rather than inside it: nested inside the 3D-rotating screen, Safari only drew it once the lid stopped.
- `public/site.css`: the ground, Figtree and the stage. `public/credits.html` uses it too.
- `public/credits.html`: the photographs and data inside the six works and on the cards. The Fish Tank list was generated from `../Sources/Atrium/Resources/reef-credits.tsv` (one line per source); regenerate it if the reef changes. CC BY requires credit on the site itself, so the download screen links to it.
- `public/fonts/`: Figtree, variable, Latin only, from Google Fonts (OFL). Self-hosted: no third-party requests.
- `public/icon.png`: the app icon at 192 px, the favicon.

## Media
Every image comes from the app's own renderer, never a screen recording, so the site shows exactly what the app draws.
- `site/media.sh [slug…]` renders each work with the snapshot test at 30 fps (`SNAPSHOT_MOVIE_FPS=30`) and its look's settings (e.g. `nebula.palette=Hubble`, `city.place=0`), crossfades the last 2 s of a 14 s render into its first 2 s so the 12 s loop has no seam, and writes AV1 and HEVC (Safari on M1 and M2 can't decode AV1) plus a JPEG poster of the first frame to `public/media/`. That folder is gitignored, so the script is the source of truth. About 1.5 minutes a work.
- Desktop is 2560 wide; phones get a 3:4 portrait slab cut around the work's focal point from `design.md`, at 1080×1440, and a 960-wide landscape copy for the laptop's screen. `site.js` picks phone media on portrait screens. The `codecs` strings in `site.js` match the levels the encodes come out at; check with `ffprobe` if the sizes change.
- Nebula rolls a new nebula every render. Re-render with `OUT=/some/dir` until a roll suits, then copy it in. Its card's preview is the canvas's roll, cut from `nebula-desktop.hevc.mp4` with media.sh's encode settings at 1280 wide, so re-make it from there after a new roll.
- `site/media.sh cards` cuts a card (640×400) for every wallpaper from the README's screenshots in `../docs/images` (`card-<slug>` cuts one); the `CARDS` list in the script picks one each. Add a line there, one in `PREVIEWS` and a `<li>` in the pane for a new wallpaper. `cards` recuts them all from the stills as they are now, so a still retaken since changes its card: Live Sky's card still has the landscape its loop has, and its README still no longer does.
- `site/media.sh previews` (or `preview-<slug>` for one) renders every wallpaper's preview: a 12 s loop like the works', in about its card's look, at 1280 wide, AV1 and HEVC plus a poster, 0.1 to 2.5 MB a copy (busy ones like Fish Tank and Deep Space Tour are the largest). Each loads only when its card is picked. The codecs string for them is in `site.js` too.
- Aurora ended the canvas for a day (Purple, at 2× speed so it visibly moved; accent `#d9b0fa`, measured like the others: the chroma-weighted mean hue of its render at oklch L 0.82, C 0.11). Dave cut it as one work too many; it's still one of the cards.
- ffmpeg and swift test read stdin; in the script's loop they must not (`-nostdin`, `</dev/null`), or they swallow the list of works.
- A work's extra environment is read as the shell reads it (`eval` in `render`), so a value with a space in it is quoted: `SNAPSHOT_DEFAULTS="…,meander.colors=Survey Blue,…"`.
- Chrome shows the loops lighter than the app draws them, or its screenshots do: Etching's dim powder is 103 of 255 in the render, in the poster and in a frame decoded by ffmpeg, and 114 in a headless Chrome screenshot of the page, where the page's own colours come out exact. That is what reading the video with BT.709's curve would give (the files are tagged with sRGB's curve and no matrix). Not looked into, and not checked on a real screen or in Safari. Contrast figures from 2026-10-13 on are against what Chrome shows.

## Access and performance
- Only works on screen play; the rest wait with `preload="none"` until they're next. Everything pauses with the tab.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` starts on the posters (Play resumes), and the dissolves stay (they're what Reduce Motion asks for). A card's preview follows Pause and Reduce Motion too, opening on its first frame; tapping the video plays or pauses it, since the Pause button is behind the pane.
- On phones the download screen's words sit below the small laptop (or, without it, over Fish Tank's open water, lifted to hold 4.5:1): Download and the pane's button share a line, and the two feature lines move into the pane (only one copy is ever displayed). The pane is darker there.
- Scrolling restyles as little as it can, and moves nothing from a script. The laptop's zoom, the fades of its notch, menu bar, Dock and opening lines, and the portrait hand-over to the full-window wallpaper are CSS animations on the page's scroll (`animation-timeline: scroll(root)`, smoothstep as `cubic-bezier(0.333, 0, 0.667, 1)`), as the labels are, so Safari from 26.4 runs them off the main thread; `laptop.js` only sets their ends (`--s0`, `--y0`, `--x0` and the last screen's), and on portrait screens picks the laptop's loop at each hand-over. The laptop and the stage are as tall as the large viewport (`100lvh`), and `laptop.js` refits only when the width or a viewport changes, so a phone's toolbars moving as it scrolls resize nothing: on an iPhone the zoom had jerked, which Dave saw (2026-10-15), and before this each toolbar move refitted the laptop mid-zoom. The moving accent is set on the site's bar, its only moving use. Main-thread style work per scroll frame (Chrome, phone-sized, CPU slowed 4×): mid-page 3.2 to 0.8 ms, during the zoom 2.4 to 0.5 ms.
- No analytics, cookies, trackers or third-party requests.

## Build and deploy
- Plain HTML, CSS and JS in `public/`: no framework, no build step, no npm packages. `npx wrangler` is the only tool. Run it from `site/` so its `.wrangler/` state lands there (gitignored).
- Pages serves static files whole (a 200, even for a range request), and Safari won't play video from a server like that. `functions/media/[[path]].js` answers byte ranges for `/media/*`; `node site/range-check.mjs` checks it. If traffic outgrows the free tier of Function calls, cache `/media` with a Cache Rule on atrium.show, or move it to R2.
- Preview: `cd site && npx wrangler pages dev public --port 8788 --compatibility-date=2026-04-01` (the installed wrangler's runtime is older than today's date). Run from `site/` it serves the Function too.
- Check every change at 1440×900 and 390×844, at several scroll positions and with reduced motion, with no console errors, and look at the screenshots. Headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol works when the Chrome extension isn't connected (launch it with `--remote-allow-origins=*`).
- Deploy only to the Cloudflare account named in `../../CLAUDE.md`, never another: wrangler is logged in to two. Pass the ID on the command line, `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID=… npx wrangler pages deploy public --project-name atrium --branch lab`, so it never lands in a file here. `--branch lab` is the preview Dave opens on his phone (lab.atrium-4pv.pages.dev); production is `main`. `atrium.show` is registered in that account (2026-10-01) and is the project's custom domain (live 2026-10-02), with `www.atrium.show` attached too. A custom domain needs its DNS record as well (a proxied CNAME to atrium-4pv.pages.dev), and wrangler's login can't edit DNS, so Dave adds that in the dashboard.
- Other Claude sessions share this working tree (see `.git/atrium-claims.md`). Commit only site files, by explicit path. The site has no version number, and never bumps the app's.
