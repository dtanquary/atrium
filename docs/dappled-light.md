# Dappled Light

Sunlight through a tree onto a warm white plaster wall (komorebi), lit by the real Sun where the viewer is. The light only falls when the Sun is on the wall's side and above the horizon, golden near sunset, gone at night but for a warm streetlight or faint moonlight at the real phase. Set to follow the live weather, it softens under cloud and the wind sets how much the leaves sway; it starts on Clear. The wall can also be terracotta limewash, clay plaster, whitewashed brick or painted lap siding, or the light can fall on an oak floor, in the open or through a window. It's the first wallpaper made for Light Mode; in Dark Mode the white plaster is charcoal.

- **Files:** `Sources/Atrium/DappledLight.swift` (the scene, its textures and its shader). `Resources/dappled-*`: each surface as greyscale HEICs (albedo, slope across and up, and height for the deep ones; brick's albedo in colour), and a leaf atlas, which the canopy and the near twig are both drawn from, credited in `dappled-credits.tsv`. It uses `SkyMath.swift` for the Sun and Moon, `Location.swift`, `LiveWeather.swift` for cloud and wind, `Atmosphere` (WeatherSky.swift) for the colour of sunlight through the air, and `CloudNoise` for smooth noise.
- **Entry:** `dappledLight(size:)` builds `final class DappledLight: SKScene`. Its entry in Scenes.swift is "Dappled Light", icon `leaf.fill`, tint `.green`, with `DappledLight.knobs` and Weather's status line (`weather.status`) under its Weather menu.
- **Kind:** one full-screen shader over baked textures; the light is worked out on the CPU once a second.

## How it works
- **The wall:** 2.4 m of wall across the screen whatever its size. By default it faces the Sun (by moonlight, the Moon): it turns with it, about 15° an hour, so the light falls whenever the Sun is up, and the dapples are round at sunrise and sunset and stretched upright at noon. It turns from Sun to Moon below the horizon, where neither light shows. Or it faces one of eight compass points, and the light only falls while the Sun is on its side. Its frame is (right, up, out) as seen standing in front of it. Every second `light()` turns the Sun (or the Moon or a lamp) into that frame: `from`, a unit vector whose `z` is the cosine of the angle the light meets the wall.
- **The surface** (`surfaces`, picked under Wall → Surface): an albedo, greyscale and tinted in linear light or in its own colours (brick), and its relief: the slope across and up, and for siding and brick the height. `textures(_:)` packs them into two textures, albedo and relief (slope, slope, height), and finds the albedo's mean, which the tint is divided by, so a tint is the surface's real average colour. Only the last surface's textures are kept, shared by every display and the Settings preview; a new pick swaps them in place, with no rebuild.
  - **White plaster:** Poly Haven's White Stucco, 2 m square, warm white (charcoal in Dark Mode).
  - **Terracotta limewash:** Poly Haven's Painted Plaster Wall, its clouding 2.5 times as strong, tinted F&B Red Earth (#BF7B69), over the white plaster's relief.
  - **Clay plaster:** Poly Haven's Patterned Clay Plaster, trowel swirls, in its own colour.
  - **Whitewashed brick:** Poly Haven's Whitewashed Brick, 2 m square, in colour: greyscale lost the red of the brick under the wash.
  - **Sage siding, Dusty blue siding:** ambientCG's WoodSiding009, 2 × 1 m, boards about 14 cm, the grain at 45% under fresh paint, tinted #7F9C8E and #9DB3C6. The scan has one joint straight down through every board, which repeated like a tile seam; it's painted out. Sage paints like SW Evergreen Fog came out khaki in afternoon sun: a sage has to lean blue to stay green under warm light.
  - **Oak floor, Oak floor by a window:** Poly Haven's Oak Wood Planks, 1.2 m square, turned so the boards run up the screen toward the light, tinted white oak (#C9A47A).
  - **Relief shades itself** where it's deep: siding's laps (1.5 cm) and brick's mortar (1.2 cm). The shader marches eight steps toward the light through the height map, so each lap shades a line under it, as wide as the lip's depth × the light's slope: wide at midday, thin when a low Sun faces the wall.
- **Floors:** a floor faces up. Its frame puts the way the wall would face (Toward the Sun, or the compass point) at the top of the screen, so the light comes from the top, and the dapples stretch up the screen by 1/sin(altitude). Every other part of the light, the leaves' layers included, works unchanged in that frame.
- **The window:** 1.2 m wide and 0.8 m tall in three by two panes, its sill 0.8 m up, in a wall past the top of the screen. The shader follows the ray from each point toward the light back to the wall and tests the panes, so the patch of light stretches as the Sun drops and shears when it's off to one side, and its edges blur by the Sun's width times the distance to the window. The view is centred on the patch, wherever the Sun puts it: around midday the whole window shows, from mid-afternoon it's longer than the screen, a band with the bars across it, and by golden hour a long amber stripe. The light only comes in with the source on the window's side. The room is lit by the sky through the window: warmer, a quarter as bright as outdoors, and up to 1.15 times nearer the window's patch, a soft glow that's all there is to see under cloud. The tree's shadow from the sky doesn't reach in.
- **Pinhole physics:** every gap between leaves is a pinhole camera, so each bright spot is an image of the Sun: its diameter is 0.0093 × the gap's distance from the wall along the ray (the Sun spans 0.53°), and it's an ellipse on the wall, longer by 1/cos(incidence) along the way the light travels. The shader works in *sky offset*: `u_proj` turns metres on the wall into how far (in Sun radii) the direction a point sees through a gap is from the Sun's centre, per metre of path. In those coordinates every image is a round disc of radius 1 whatever the angle, so the stretch, the size and the eclipse all come for free, and the lookups stay cheap.
- **Layers of the tree**, each shifted by the Sun as a shadow of something that far out from the wall would be (`shift`, depth × tan): so as the Sun crosses the sky the shadows crawl across the wall, the near twig least.
  - **The far crown, 5 m out:** its shadow is a soft mass (the canopy texture's green, clumps of real leaves blurred by the 2 cm the Sun blurs them at that distance), and inside it the gaps make dapples (`gap`, called from `main` over the 3 × 3 cells of sky offset around each pixel): at most one gap to a 2 × 2 cell, each 2.3–9 m out, spread evenly in log (so its image is 0.45–1.8 radii across: far gaps make big images, near ones small). Each image is a limb-darkened disc (1 − 0.6(1 − μ)), blurred by the gap's own size (Minnaert: "for a hazy picture the size of the aperture must be added"), and overlaps add.
    - **Where the gaps are:** a gap opens in a cell when its hash passes a threshold, 0.78 − 0.4 × how thin the crown is, so more toward the crown's edge (22–62% of cells, as before), swung ±0.2 (one standard deviation) by the canopy texture's alpha, value noise over 30 and 15 cm laid out with the tree: the gaps gather in groups that merge into lumps, with bare shade between. Both are read once for each gap, where its image falls (sky offset mapped back to the wall by the sprays' blur matrix, × 5/1.2), so a gap is there or not across its whole disc. Read at each pixel, as the first version did, the threshold followed the crown's contours across the discs and cut them into hard-edged crescents. Only gaps whose image could reach the pixel are looked up, which keeps it to one texture read for most of the nine.
    - **How bright:** a gap's image is as bright as the gap's area over the image's, (r / size)², so a wide gap near the wall makes a bright, soft blob and a pinhole far out a faint, crisp disc. Physically that spans a factor of a hundred; photos show the visible dapples at 0.7–1.6 times the median spot, so it's squeezed into 0.2–1 of full sun, linear in r / size between 0.05 and 0.6. Before, size and brightness were separate random numbers.
    - **Slits:** a third of the gaps are slits between leaves, whose image is the disc smeared along them: an ellipse up to 1.6 times as long, at any angle, held short enough to stay inside the cells searched.
    - **Breathing:** with any wind, each gap opens and closes by up to 20% (its radius, and so its brightness and blur), at 0.4–0.9 rad/s in its own phase, so the dapples don't slide as one fixed sheet. It runs on `u_now` itself, slow enough not to coarsen over three days; on the flutter's hour-wrapped clock every gap jumped by up to 15% once an hour. Calm (Sway 0) holds them still.
    - The first version was dapples alone, a field of polka dots; the reference photos showed they only appear inside the crown's shade, among leaf shadows. Confined to the shade, they were still near-identical discs one to a jittered cell, polka dots in the shade (the review of 2026-11-14); the clumps, the spread of sizes tied to brightness, the slits and the breathing are its fix.
  - **Sprays of leaves, 1.2 m out** (the canopy texture's red): twigs off a few wandering branches, real leaves set alternately along each twig and one at its tip. Their shadow is blurred over the source's image with seven taps (`u_blur` maps sky offset back to the wall, so the blur is the right ellipse), each tap dropped where the Moon covers the Sun, so an eclipse bites their soft edges too. The texture is pre-blurred by a texel so thin twigs don't ghost into double lines between the taps. Turning the tap pattern per pixel hid the ghosting too, but cost 2.2 ms of GPU by thrashing the texture cache.
  - **A twig near the wall, 0.25 m out:** a sprig of the canopy's own tree (`twig(_:of:using:)`), drawn each launch with the canopy: a gently bowed stem, tapering from 7 to 2.5 mm, with the atlas's leaves of that kind, maple's in two opposite pairs, oak's five crowding toward the tip, beech's alternate along it with one at the tip. They're shade leaves, low in the tree, so a little bigger than the crown's (maple 20–25 cm with its long petiole, oak 17–21, beech 13–16), and each is foreshortened across to 50–100% as it tilts out of the wall's plane. Its texture is 0.75 m square at 1024², blurred by the 2.3 mm the Sun blurs a shadow 25 cm from the wall. A sharp shadow, swaying about its base, from one of four places, rolled for each scene (so each display, and a rebuild, may differ): hanging in from the top right or top left, or reaching in and up from the right or left edge. Its place is fixed when the scene is built, so it starts there and then drifts with the Sun.
    - Before (to 2026-11-15) it was one scan, ambientCG LeafSet005's forked twig with three big serrated leaves, the same three in every still and always top right, under a maple, oak or beech alike, with pinholes in its matte showing as white hairlines. The review suggested painting the holes out, shipping a twig for each tree and rolling its place; no set but LeafSet005 has a twig, so the twigs are drawn from the leaves already in the atlas instead, and the scan is gone (55 KB less to download).
    - The review also suggested letting the leaf blade pass 12–18% of the direct light, veins and stem opaque. Not done: a leaf's 5–15% transmission comes out diffuse, spread over a hemisphere, so from 25 cm off the wall it adds about T × Ω/π ≈ 0.3% of sunlight behind a 10 cm leaf, a fiftieth of the shade's own skylight. A real near leaf's shadow is as dark as any other shade, which the photos showed.
  - The canopy texture (1024², 3 m square, tiling) is laid out afresh each launch from one kind of tree: maple, oak or beech.
- **Light and colour:** sunlight is `Atmosphere.sunlight` squared (twice the path, since the low air is hazier than the model's clean air; single strength gave a pale yellow sunset where photos are amber), times a fade from 0.12 down to 0.02 of the cosine so grazing light fades out rather than stopping at an edge. Skylight is blue by day (0.8, 0.88, 1) at 0.075·√(sin h + 0.02), paler blue through twilight, down to the glow of towns (3e−7 of the Sun). Sunlight bounced off the ground adds 0.05·(1 − sin h)³ of it to the shade, which warms it at golden hour, when photos show warm shade, and leaves it blue by day. The plaster's grain is lit by its normal map, so a low Sun rakes across it.
- **The sky's shadow of the tree:** the crown also hides part of the sky, so the skylight falling on the wall is up to 50% less where the tree is: a soft shadow straight back from the crown's real place (0.2 m low, since the sky is brightest overhead), from the canopy texture's blue, the crown blurred about 12 cm. Under cloud, in fog, with the Sun behind the wall, and by night it's all there is to see. The real sky would blur a crown 5 m out over metres into an even grey; the first version did (20% of very smooth noise), and on a foggy dawn Dave saw "a grey screen" and thought it was broken.
- **Exposure:** the eye adapts: the wall's brightness follows the light only as its 0.18th power, and dims a further 50% at night. It's worked out from the light before clouds, so a cloud passing still dims the wall, as it does to the eye. Tone-mapped like Weather: `sqrt(1 − exp(−x))`.
- **Night:** below 1.5° under the horizon the source becomes the Moon, 2.5 millionths of sunlight at full, falling off as the lit fraction to the 2.5; each gap's image is then the Moon's own phase (`u_src.w` is the cosine of the phase angle, `u_term` points to the bright limb), and the colours shift blue (the Purkinje shift). With Streetlight chosen, a sodium-orange lamp (1, 0.55, 0.2) up to the left instead, 0.8° across (a 20 cm globe 12 m away), on from 1.5° to 4° below the horizon, with only a third of the blue shift. Lamplight is bright for its size, so the eye dims a further 25% under it; before that the lamplit wall looked like a dull afternoon.
- **Dark Mode** (`systemIsDark`, read when the scene is built; the app rebuilds it when the appearance changes): the white plaster becomes the same stucco in charcoal (the other surfaces keep their own colours), (0.060, 0.066, 0.074) in linear light, the colour of Poly Haven's Plastered Wall 05, in exactly the same light. Sunlit patches reach a warm mid-grey (about sRGB 137, 123, 103 at 4:30 pm) over near-black shade, as on the dark walls in the reference photos (lit #64605b over #1c2119); at golden hour the wall goes copper. By night a charcoal wall would be black, so in Dark Mode the sky's glow on it is five times as strong (only the ambient light: boosting the exposure instead lit the streetlight to daylight).
- **Eclipses:** by day `light()` places the Moon over the Sun as seen from here (topocentric, `Sky.moon(_:latitude:longitude:)`, good to 10″; the low-precision series it replaced was 10–30 minutes out and missed totality). While the discs overlap, `u_src` holds the Moon's offset and radius in Sun radii, every dapple becomes the same crescent (flipped through its gap), the soft leaves' taps drop the covered part, and the light dims by the area covered. At annularity every spot becomes a ring. The Moon at night, or the lamp, shows no eclipse. Preview an eclipse fakes a 70% one.
- **Weather** (`LiveWeather`, shared with Weather): clear (codes 0–1 under 30% cloud) keeps the Sun out; broken cloud (code 2, 0–1 over 30%, and showers 80–82) sends it in and out, for as much of the time as the sky is covered, on a 70 s value noise, the dapples blurring into shade as the cloud thickens (`u_wind.z`); anything else (overcast, fog, drizzle, rain, snow, storms) is diffuse light only, grey and even. Wind sways the branches: 1 cm at 10 km/h, growing as wind^1.4 up to 5 cm, in gusts (a 12 s noise), at 0.14–0.43 Hz in two sines per axis with a phase that varies over the wall. Above 5 km/h two in five dapples start to blink (flutter), up to 35% by 20 km/h. The twig turns by up to 0.7° at 10 km/h. Research: branch modes are about 1 Hz and whole trees 0.15–0.5 Hz; leaf flutter is 3–8 Hz. Real flutter at 3–8 Hz looked like flicker, so the blink is 2.4–5.6 Hz and small. It runs on `mod(u_now, 3600)`, as twinkles do: at 15–35 rad/s it was the first thing to coarsen as `u_now` grew over three days.

## Tuning constants
- Wall 2.4 m across; crown 5 m out, sprays 1.2 m, twig 0.25 m; path length clamped at cos 0.25. On a floor the same distances are heights above it.
- The twig's places (fraction of the wall across and up from the middle, and the way it reaches, 0 up): (0.42, 0.33) at 2.55 rad, (−0.42, 0.33) at −2.55, (0.5, −0.12) at 0.95, (−0.5, −0.08) at −0.95.
- Window 1.2 × 0.8 m, sill 0.8 m, bars 4 cm; the room's light 0.25–1.15 of the sky's, tinted (1, 0.9, 0.78).
- Pinholes: a gap in a cell when `h.z > 0.78 − 0.4·open + 0.6·(clump − 0.5)` (faded in over ±0.03), `open` and `clump` read where the image falls; size 0.45·4^u; radius (0.1 + 0.3·h.z)·(0.5 + 0.5·open) of the image, × 0.8–1 breathing; brightness 0.2–1 by r / size; a third stretched up to 1.6×.
- Exposure `1.8·(L/0.33)^−0.82/0.33`, × (1 − 0.5·night).

## Performance
- Release build, 2x, 1512×982, 2026-09-26, with other sessions' tests running: 0.41–0.54 ms CPU, 0.57–0.99 ms GPU. The dapples cost most: nine gap images per pixel inside the crown.
- 2026-10-06, debug test build, 2x, each surface at 4 pm and 5:45 pm: 0.51–0.74 ms CPU, 0.47–0.91 ms GPU. The siding and brick's relief march (eight more texture reads) doesn't show above the noise.
- 2026-11-15, debug test build, 2x, white plaster, back to back with other sessions building: the clumped gaps cost 0.03–0.07 ms of GPU more than the polka dots (0.73–0.79 → 0.79–0.86 ms at 2:30 pm and 4:06 pm, which in mid-November stand in for September's 4 pm and 5:45 pm). The clumps' noise adds 9 ms to the canopy's build (release), once a launch, and drawing the twig 20 ms; its lookup costs what the scan's did.
- **With no direct light it draws only the skylight** (2026-11-15): overcast, the source behind the wall or below the horizon, `u_light` is zero, and the shader skips the crown, its gaps, the sprays, the twig and the relief's march, all of which it would multiply by 0. GPU 0.79–0.80 → 0.29–0.40 ms overcast, 0.61 → 0.22–0.31 ms on a north wall in the afternoon (debug test build, back to back, other sessions building); lit frames cost the same. As Sources/CLAUDE.md warns, the branch moved about 200 of the 5.9 million pixels of a lit frame by 1 of 255 (2 on the siding), under the shader's own dither of ±0.5; unlit frames are the same to the byte.
- Textures: the surface 32 MB (albedo and relief, each 2048² RGBA; the siding's half that), the canopy 4 MB. Built when shown, shared by every display and the Settings preview, and let go with the last scene showing them; only one surface at a time. The canopy is kept for the launch, since its kind of tree is rolled once a launch.

- **On battery it runs at 20 fps at most while direct light casts the leaves' shadows, and 10 with none** (`batteryRate`, set in `light()` every second; 2026-10-13). In the default Clear weather the branches sway about 1 cm at 2.7 rad/s, about 42 device pixels (2x on a 14-inch MacBook Pro, 3024 × 1964) a second on the wall: 2.1 px a frame at 20 fps; the leaves flutter at 2.4–5.6 Hz, more than a third of 15. Direct light is the Sun's by day, and at night the streetlight's (At night, on by default) or the Moon's; overcast, or with the source behind the wall or below the horizon, only the skylight's soft shade sways. On a windy live day, or at Sway 3×, the sway reaches 200–600 px a second.
- **On battery it draws the wall at three quarters of the screen's resolution each way** (`batteryScale` in Scenes.swift, under the same condition as its battery rate, with Settings → Power's Lighter drawing on battery, on unless changed; 2026-10-14). That cuts the GPU's work a frame by 32% (0.75 to 0.51 ms, frames drawn back to back at 3024 × 1964), about 15 ms of GPU time a second at 20 fps. Side by side at 100% (`trimShots` in TrimShots.swift) the shadows can't be told apart; at half resolution the plaster's grain softened.

## Gotchas and shortcuts
- `ponytail:` **the lamp is at infinity.** A real streetlight's rays spread about ±7° across the wall; here they're parallel.
- `ponytail:` **a bigger source means fewer, bigger gaps.** The gaps are laid out in sky offset, so the lamp (3× the Sun) gets a coarser crown. Pinholes fixed in the tree would be better if the lamp ever matters.
- `ponytail:` **the gaps' layout follows the light.** Hashing the gaps in sky offset means the lattice slowly shears as the Sun moves; at a few mm a minute beside the Sun's own 2 cm drift it can't be seen.
- `ponytail:` **the sky's shadow is sharper than the sky would make it** (see above), so the tree still reads without sun.
- `ponytail:` **the view follows the window's patch of light.** A real room's patch slides across the floor through the day and is mostly somewhere else; here it stays mid-screen. A fixed camera would need a much wider view.
- `ponytail:` **a floor keeps the wall's leaf layers.** The crown 5 m above the floor and a twig 25 cm above it stand in for a tree outside the window and a plant on the sill.
- **Don't randomise texture lookups per pixel** (see the sprays above): 2.2 ms. The gaps' nine lookups are fine: every pixel near a gap reads the same texel, and only gaps whose image could reach the pixel read at all (without that gate, +0.3 ms).
- The relief is greyscale HEICs packed into one texture at load: a colour HEIC's 4:2:0 chroma wrecks packed channels (correlation 0.33 back), and the PNG is 8 MB. A colour albedo (brick) is fine as a colour HEIC.

## Checked against real photos
A research agent gathered 44 reference photos of leaf shadows on walls (scratchpad `dappled/reference`, with `measurements.json`), and measured them in linear light: lit and shade colour, their ratio, lit fraction, dapple size, ellipticity, edge softness and spot-to-spot brightness. The same `measure()` runs on renders, so they compared number for number, 2026-09-26 (a clear 4:30 pm on a south-west wall at 40°N):

| | photos (median) | render |
|---|---|---|
| lit / shade, linear light | 7.7 midday, 4.1 golden | 5.4 at 4:30 pm; 3.8 at 1 pm, when the light is oblique |
| shade's blue, (B/R shade) / (B/R lit) | 1.42 midday, 1.07 golden | 1.5 |
| dapple size / frame width (area-weighted) | 0.035 | 0.030 |
| edge 10–90% / spot diameter | 0.17 | 0.14 |
| spot brightness, p10–p90 of median | 0.72–1.57 | 0.73–1.38 |
| lit fraction | 0.35 in dapple photos, 0.66 with near leaves | 0.55 |

The render column predates the dapples' rework of 2026-11-15 and wasn't measured again (`measure()` stayed in the September scratchpad): the brightness spread of the gaps was matched to the old one's, and the mean brightness of the stills moved by 1%.

The shade is kept a little lighter than the photos (whose own highlights clip, so their ratios understate), since calm here means soft. Before calibration the ratio was 3, every dapple equally bright, and three times too many of them.

What the photos showed besides:
- The wall is mostly lit, crossed by recognisable leaf and branch shadows; round dapples appear inside the denser shade, not as a field of their own. The first render was all dapples.
- Shade on a white wall is blue-grey; sunlit plaster is warm; at golden hour the light is deep amber and the shadows lavender.
- Near leaves are crisp, far ones melt into soft masses.

## Dave's feedback and decisions
- **The brief** (2026-09-26): the real sun at his location, a wall-facing knob, light that follows the Sun's height, moonlight at the real phase or a warm streetlight, a time preview; pinhole images of the right size and stretch, sharp near leaves and soft far ones, crescents in a real eclipse; cloud softening the dapples and wind setting the sway; compared with photos side by side; "calm means soft and dim too".
- **Dark Mode** (2026-09-26): shown the same scene at 4:30 pm on a white wall exposed down like a dim room, charcoal plaster, dark green plaster and terracotta, and the night look, beside reference photos of dark walls, he picked charcoal plaster (my recommendation: it keeps the real light and the physics).

- **"A grey screen"** (2026-09-27, 6:57 am): fog (0.1 km visibility), the Sun a few minutes up in the east behind his south-west wall, in Dark Mode: really no direct light, so a flat charcoal wall. That led to the sky's shadow of the tree, and to Toward the Sun, which he chose as the default over keeping the real south-west wall or ignoring the weather by day.

- **Streetlight by default** (2026-09-27): "the default for night should be street light not moon light".
- **Clear by default** (2026-09-27): "so that first impressions are good if the user is having a cloudy day". Live weather is one menu pick away.

- **More surfaces** (2026-10-06): asked for more backgrounds, "like a hardwood floor, or siding on a house in a nice color, not sure if we want concrete". A research agent ranked surfaces from leaf-shadow photography and design (limewash and lap siding first; zellige, red brick and board-formed concrete too busy; under LRV 25 too dark, over 85 blows out). Shown 15 surfaces rendered under one tree (blush plaster, terracotta limewash, clay, whitewashed brick, three siding colours, white vertical boards, an oak floor with and without a window, tatami, smooth and board-formed concrete, shoji), he picked terracotta limewash, clay, whitewashed brick, sage and dusty blue siding, and the oak floor with and without the window, and left concrete out (smooth read as the white plaster in warm sun). The first floor mock-ups were lit as a wall facing the Sun; a side agent flagged it, and Dave saw them again lit as a real floor before choosing ("they all look good, so both"). In Dark Mode the new surfaces keep their own colours.

- **The review of 2026-11-14** (every wallpaper, read from code and stills): inside the crown's shade the dapples read as polka dots, and the near twig as a sticker. Dave asked for all of its suggestions. The dapples now gather, vary in size with brightness tied to it, smear along slits and breathe with the wind; the near twig is a sprig of the canopy's own tree, in one of four places; and with no direct light the shader skips the leaves' work (above). Filming its loop and menu tile in Light Mode waits for Dave to approve the look.

## Checking it
- `SNAPSHOT_SCENE="Dappled Light" SNAPSHOT_DEFAULTS="dappled.previewTime=1,dappled.previewHour=16.5,dappled.weather=1" swift test` renders a clear afternoon on a south-west wall at the test's fallback location (40°N, 90°W). Try 13 for midday, 18.3 for golden hour, 19 for dusk, and 22 with `dappled.night=1` for the streetlight. Add `dappled.surface=N` (0–7, in the menu's order) for another surface; the window floor (7) also wants `dappled.weather=3` for its overcast glow. Add `dappled.previewEclipse=1` for crescents, and `SNAPSHOT_APPEARANCE=light|dark` for the white or charcoal wall. The layout of leaves is random each launch, so roll a few for screenshots; `dappled.seed=N` (any whole number but 0, for the tests and nothing else) rolls the same tree every time, so two renders differ only by the code.
- `swift test --filter eclipsesLineUp` checks the Moon against four real eclipses; `dappledLightSurfacesLoad` checks every surface finds its scans; `dappledLightCanopyClumps` that the canopy carries its clumps; `dappledLightTwigFits` that every kind of twig stays inside its texture. `dappled.seed` also picks the twig's place (the seed's remainder by 4, in the order above).
