# Wallpaper ideas

Ideas for new wallpapers, kept here so they aren't lost. They came out of a brainstorm with Dave on 2026-10-10, aimed at what has worked so far: real data, photoreal, soft and dim, and engines the app already has. Three were picked that day and have been built ([Built](#built)). **None of the rest is researched** but Hourglass (2026-11-18), whose two reports are linked from its entry. Every data feed, licence and cost under Parked is a guess to check before a build, and a big one starts with mock-ups for Dave to pick from.

When one is picked, it gets its own doc, and that doc is where it is kept from then on.

## Built
Picked by Dave on 2026-10-10, researched and mocked up that day, and built on 2026-10-11 and 12. All three were hidden behind the `unfinished` switch while Dave tested them, and have been in the app for everyone since 0.109.0 (2026-10-12), when he said to unhide them and put them on the README and the site. Each one's doc has what was built, what was measured and what is still wrong.

- **Black Hole.** A lensed accretion disc slowly turning, bending the real Milky Way and catalogue stars behind it; one shader, beside Nebula and Galaxy. Three disc colours, a size slider from a small hole in a wide sky to a close-up wider than the screen, stars that twinkle and a sky that can drift. A wallpaper of its own: [black-hole.md](black-hole.md).
- **Pixel Night Train.** A ground-level window seat: country sliding past on the real clock, with rain on the glass when it rains where you are. Dave picked it as an addition to [Pixel Window Seat](pixel-window-seat.md) rather than a wallpaper of its own, so it is that wallpaper's third window ("Window on: A train"), with three carriages and seven sceneries: [pixel-window-seat/train.md](pixel-window-seat/train.md).
- **Meander.** A river migrating across its floodplain, cutting off oxbows and leaving scroll bars, drawn as lidar relief or as an old map after Harold Fisk's 1944 sheets of the Mississippi (US Army Corps of Engineers, public domain). A new river on each load, in one colour family. A wallpaper of its own: [meander.md](meander.md).

## Asked for outright
- **Etching.** Dave asked for it on 2026-10-12, with research and mock-ups first: old etchings, engravings and woodcuts drawn slowly in one unbroken line on a screen of grey powder, after the two-dial drawing toy. Built that day and hidden behind the `unfinished` switch while he tests it: [etching.md](etching.md). A frame with dials round the screen, and more pictures, are its own next ideas.
- **Peg Board (cut on 2026-11-14).** Dave asked on 2026-10-12, with research and mock-ups first, for a clone of the peg-light toy of 1967, "with lights, showing various lighting patterns and what not". A black sheet of holes with glowing coloured pegs placed one at a time: a grown snow crystal or a picture built outline first, then a slow light show, then the sheet clears. Built the same day and hidden as `unfinished` until he had tried it, then shown to everyone from 0.111.0. He cut it a month later: the idea itself didn't appeal to him on his desktop (the Cut note in [README.md](README.md#daves-direction-so-far)).
- **Ant Colony.** Dave asked on 2026-10-12, again with research and mock-ups first, for a colony of ants seen from the side through glass, "as it slowly forms and gets bigger". A nest dug through the day by the wall clock, a new one each morning, in blue gel lit from its base or in sand, with ants that walk on the tunnels' walls and carry out what is dug. Built the same day and hidden as `unfinished` until he has tried it: [ant-colony.md](ant-colony.md). An ant drawn by one shader (for hundreds of them) and ants on the glass seen from above are its own next ideas.
- **A bonsai by a window.** Dave's pick for later, on 2026-09-26, of three ways to show "one tree that lives on my desktop through the real seasons": a maple bonsai on a bench seen through a window, wintering outdoors to go dormant. He took the lone tree on a hill first, which became A Tree for the Year and was cut on 2026-11-14: a small drawn tree never stood up to its photo hill. Close up, leaves are big on screen, where photo leaf cards hold. The oak's calendar (`Phenology`) is still in the app for Pixel Railway; the growth and bake are in git history.

- **Jellyfish.** Dave ran it through the new-wallpaper process on 2026-11-07: the shader-drawn version that "Left out on purpose" below left room for, "a shader-drawn bell on black, dim and bioluminescent". Researched and mocked up that day, built behind the `unfinished` switch, and shown to everyone from 0.122.0 (2026-11-09): [jellyfish.md](jellyfish.md).
- **Kelp Forest.** Dave asked on 2026-11-08: "underwater kelp, start with mockups and come up with a high fidelity design for me and we go from there". Giant kelp seen from inside the forest, swaying in the swell, drawn by shaders rather than cut out of photos, where a Kelp Forest tank of photo cut-outs had been dropped from the Fish Tank. Researched and mocked up that day; from two rounds he chose giant kelp, level and looking up, in three lights, and it was built behind the `unfinished` switch and shown to everyone from 0.122.0 (2026-11-09): [kelp-forest.md](kelp-forest.md).
- **Canopy.** Pick #3 of the ideas page of 2026-11-14 ("Lying on the forest floor, looking straight up"), which Dave ran through the new-wallpaper process on 2026-11-16: broadleaf and birch crowns overhead through the real seasons, stars in the gaps at night. Researched and mocked up that day; of round 1 he said "A/C/F look the best, build from there", a real bare stand with scanned leaves grown on it, and it is being built behind the `unfinished` switch: [canopy.md](canopy.md).
- **Loom.** An idea from the ideas page of 2026-11-14 ("Cloth growing row by row, its pattern appearing as it is woven, then rolling on to the next"), which Dave ran through the new-wallpaper process on 2026-11-18, noting Peg Board, which it built on, is cut for good. Researched and mocked up that day from real drafts of 1928; round 1 waits on his picks: [loom.md](loom.md).
- **Saturn's Rings.** From the ideas page of 2026-11-14 ("Drifting just above the ring plane: thousands of fine bands, the planet's shadow lying across them, and a moon's ripple passing through"), which Dave ran through the new-wallpaper process on 2026-11-18. One shader in Black Hole's family, from Cassini's real ring profiles, in today's real sunlight. Researched and mocked up that evening; round 1 is with him: [saturns-rings.md](saturns-rings.md).
- **Seismograph.** On the ideas page of 2026-11-14 ("A paper drum with a pen crawling across it, one line each half hour and a day to a sheet"), which Dave ran through the new-wallpaper process on 2026-11-18: the ground's motion at the nearest open seismic station, drawn line by line on Etching's paper, the quakes of the whole Earth as trains of slow waves. Researched and mocked up that day; round 1 is with him: [seismograph.md](seismograph.md).
- **Beehive.** Pick #5 of the ideas page of 2026-11-14 ("An observation hive behind glass. Comb is drawn out cell by cell through the day, filled with honey and capped, with bees working across it"), which Dave ran through the new-wallpaper process on 2026-11-18: one comb face-on, drawn out and filled on the wall clock as Ant Colony's nest is dug, the bees gathering when it is cold where you are. Researched and mocked up that day: [beehive.md](beehive.md).

## Parked
In the order they were proposed, most promising first.

- **Valley Fog.** Layered photo ridgelines at dawn with fog flowing slowly between them, lit by Weather's sky. The same cut-out-and-relight approach as Aurora and Weather. It could thicken with the live humidity. The softest, dimmest idea here, which is what Zen Garden wasn't.
- **The Planets, live.** Jupiter as a telescope shows it now: NASA maps lit by a shader, as The Moon does, with the four big moons and their shadows in their real places. Jupiter turns in under ten hours, so it is visibly alive. Saturn with its real ring tilt and Mars could follow. It would nest in Solar System Tour beside The Moon and The Sun.
- **Earth Today.** Real full-disc photos from the weather satellite over the user's hemisphere, played as the last day's loop, as The Sun does with SDO's. NOAA's GOES images are public domain and come every ten minutes; cover and licences outside the Americas (Himawari, Meteosat) need checking. It could nest in Earth from Orbit.
- **Open Sea.** Ocean to the horizon under Weather's sky, with the real Moon and its glitter path, and swell (height, period, direction) from Open-Meteo's marine feed for the nearest coast. The risk is water that looks real inside the 2 ms GPU budget. A night shore with glowing surf was tried on its own as Sea Sparkle and cut (see below).
- **Cloud Chamber.** Particle tracks condensing in mist: short fat alphas, thin wandering betas, straight muons, in their real proportions, with a slider for how busy it is. Cheap to draw, and it belongs with Schlieren and Turing Patterns. Crystals showed that correct physics doesn't make a thing pretty, so it needs mock-ups first.
- **Ink.** Suminagashi: rings of ink floating on water, slowly stirred. It is flat by nature, so Schlieren's fluid solver is physically right for it. Flowing Gradient's and Lava Lamp's family, with palettes. Incense smoke on black is a near relation on the same solver.
- **Hourglass (researched, parked by Dave on 2026-11-18).** He asked for "a wallpaper that makes my wallpaper an hourglass, where a slow steady stream of sand is falling and building up in my background, eventually filling and starting over", and to be told whether it was a good idea first. Two research reports came back that day, the only parked idea here that has them: [hourglass/research-method.md](hourglass/research-method.md) and [hourglass/research-look.md](hourglass/research-look.md), with a probe of the pile (`pile.py`, `bake.swift`). They agreed on the whole screen as one flat glass (sand between two panes, the neck mid-screen, the upper half draining into a V while the lower builds by slides that lay nested chevrons of coloured sand), since a screen that was only the lower chamber would start every fill empty. It would be cheap: baked once a fill off the main thread and compared with the clock, as Etching and Ant Colony are (0.2 to 0.45 s a bake, measured; GPU estimated well under budget; 80 to 200 MB of textures). What stopped it: the probe's pile is two straight-sided triangles that read as vector art, a sand-art frame risks Peg Board's fault (a toy's picture), pale sand over half the screen is Zen Garden's, the physics gives a 5-to-60-minute glass where calm wanted a day (a day-long glass would sit still for half an hour between slides), and an hourglass reads as time running out. Having read that, Dave: "lets save our research but not build this yet, doesnt sound like a great idea at the moment". Not mocked up. If it comes back, round 1 would be the looks (flat glass in Lamplight, Gypsum and Desert layers; a blown-glass close-up), films of one slide and of 15- and 60-minute fills, and the fade against a real turn.
- **Snowfall.** Fireflies' engine (a photo ground, particles in depth, fog, wind) with snow in a forest at blue hour, or under a lamp. It could follow the live weather.

## Left out on purpose
- **Jellyfish, koi, a fish school:** big animals, or nearly. Photo cut-outs of big animals never moved convincingly in the Fish Tank's Ocean Voyager and Shallow Reef, cut on 2026-10-07. Jellyfish would have to be drawn by a shader, not cut out, to be worth another try.
- **Sea Sparkle, a bioluminescent shoreline:** built on 2026-11-08 and cut the same night, "just does not look good at all compared to the others". Surf drawn by one shader read as glowing stripes on a flat sea. Breaking waves would need real faces, curls and foam (a heightfield or filmed surf), which the budget didn't leave room for. Its research and mock-ups are in git history before the cut (1028d91).
- **Soap film:** its interference colours are the ones that went olive once Crystals was dimmed for calm.
- **Ferrofluid, sand patterns (Chladni figures):** bright and sharp, as Zen Garden was.
- **A fireplace or a candle:** Campfire was cut on 2026-11-14, because flames from a simulation never looked real beside its photo (the Cut note in [README.md](README.md#daves-direction-so-far)). A fire needs footage of a real one first.
- **Live flights, live lightning, earthquakes:** as far as I know (unchecked) the flight and lightning feeds restrict reuse, and earthquakes are too sparse to be lively. Pixel City's Airport and Earth from Orbit's storms already cover the first two. Earthquakes as a map of events, that is: a seismograph's continuous trace of the ground is not sparse, and Dave ran it on 2026-11-18 ([seismograph.md](seismograph.md)).
