# Saturn's Rings

Drifting just above Saturn's rings: thousands of fine bands running off to a horizon, the planet's straight-edged shadow lying across them, the planet itself rising at one side, and a moon's ripple passing through. The bands are the rings' real structure, measured by Cassini as it watched stars pass behind them, and the light is the Sun's real angle on the rings today. One shader, in Black Hole's family, but lit by reflected sunlight rather than glowing.

The idea is from the ideas page of 2026-11-14 ("Saturn's Rings · New wallpaper · Part new": "Drifting just above the ring plane: thousands of fine bands, the planet's shadow lying across them, and a moon's ripple passing through. What is real: the rings' real structure from Cassini's measurements. Builds on: Black Hole. One shader, in Black Hole's family. Risk: low. It has to look different enough from Black Hole's disc. The ring data's licence is unchecked."). Dave ran it through the new-wallpaper process on 2026-11-18. It was researched that evening by two agents and mocked up for him to choose from, and on 2026-11-19 he chose every view, with the camera gliding between them ([Dave's decisions](#daves-decisions-2026-11-19)).

- **Research:** [the method](saturns-rings/research-method.md) (geometry, the shader, aliasing, the ring data, the moon's ripple, motion, cost, with a probe's measurements) and [the look](saturns-rings/research-look.md) (Cassini's pictures by PIA number, colours by ring and face, palettes, brightness caps, licences, names).
- **Data:** Cassini UVIS stellar-occultation profiles of the rings' optical depth, and Voyager's lit-face brightness profile, from NASA's PDS Ring-Moon Systems Node. NASA's science data policy (SPD-41a) puts mission data in the public domain: **checked** ([the policy](https://science.nasa.gov/spd-41/)). The node's own pages state no licence, so the datasets are named and credited.
- **Scripts:** `docs/saturns-rings/`: `profile.py` bakes the profile, `mock.swift` draws the mock-ups with SpriteKit's SKRenderer as the render test draws a wallpaper (meant to carry into the build), `variants.py` and `sheet.py` make the sheets.
- **Pictures of it:** the mock-up sheets in `docs/saturns-rings/`, below.
- **Where it stands (2026-11-20):** the build order's four steps are built, hidden behind the `unfinished` switch: the six views in the real light of the day, from the lit face (the standard since 0.130.13) or the unlit, each drifting slowly while it holds, the camera gliding from one to another at random, holding the one picked in Settings, or flying one smooth loop through all six at an even pace (Fly Through, 0.130.14), under the real stars and Milky Way. Since 0.130.15 the face can also change on its own, the camera flying through the ring plane out past the rings: Alternate, or lit in Light Mode and unlit in Dark Mode. Next, Dave's look at the crossings on his desktop; spokes are still his to decide.

## What the real thing looks like
From the two reports, cut to what a build would use.
- **Sizes** (NSSDC and the Ring-Moon Systems Node, checked): Saturn 60,268 km across the equator to its cloud tops, 54,364 km to the pole. The C ring 74,491–91,975 km from the centre, the B ring to 117,570, the Cassini Division to 122,050, the A ring to 136,770, with the Encke gap (322 km wide, Pan in it) at 133,584 and the Keeler gap (35 km, Daphnis in it) at 136,504; the F ring at 140,224. The rings are 5 to 30 m thick: a plane, from any view here.
- **Orbits:** 5.8 hours at the C ring's inner edge to 14.3 at the A ring's outer edge, 22.6 to 16.7 km/s.
- **The Sun now:** Saturn's equinox was 6 May 2025. On 2026-11-18 the Sun is about 8.4° south of the ring plane (from JPL Horizons, derived), so the south face is lit, and dimly; it climbs to about 27° at the 2032 solstice. The planet's shadow reaches past every ring in every season but the year round a solstice.
- **Lit face and unlit face swap which ring is bright:** from the sunlit side the B ring is the brightest (luma 203 against the A ring's 141 and the C ring's 59, PIA06485); from the unlit side it is the darkest (18 against 76–98 and 51, PIA08388), because it is too thick to let light through.
- **Colour:** pale and barely saturated. The lit B ring is cream to tan (hue 26–51°, saturation 0.15–0.3), the A ring greyer, the Cassini Division and C ring a dark cool grey. Black Hole's disc is orange at saturation 0.57. The planet is buttercream (#a89671 to #d1bb97), its night side lit a dim amber by the rings.
- **Contrast:** broad plateaus, each gently banded (±8–20% within a ring), with a few sharp steps between rings (3 to 6 times). The planet's shadow is black, with an edge about 100 km soft.
- **A moon's ripple:** Daphnis's waves on the edges of the Keeler gap (about 165 km apart, 0.5–1.5 km tall) and Pan's wakes on the Encke gap (about 1,520 km apart along the edge) are real and move with their moons, but they are under a pixel from any view that also shows the planet. Spokes (faint radial smudges thousands of km long, over the B ring, coming and going in hours) are seasonal and strongest near equinox, which is now.

## How it works
The plan, from the method research and Dave's choices. Where the build goes another way it will say so in a line beginning **As built**.
- **One shader.** A ray per pixel meets the ring plane (its radius picks the band) and the planet, an oblate sphere. The planet's shadow on the rings and the rings' shadow on the planet are worked out per pixel; light through the rings follows their optical depth, so stars and the planet show through the thin C ring and the Cassini Division. Lit and unlit faces follow the textbook single-scattering formulas for a thin layer.
- **No shimmer:** the bands are a function of radius alone, so each pixel takes the exact average of the profile over the stretch of radius it covers, from a table of averages at every width (a mip chain by radius). A table of running sums failed in SpriteKit for lack of precision; this one works and costs two reads a value.
- **Cost:** 0.27–0.32 ms of GPU a frame at 3024 × 1964 in the probe, against 1.74 for Nebula run the same way; expect 0.35–0.5 in the app with stars and the light on the planet's night side.
- **The views are cameras, not scenes.** Each view is a camera (distance from Saturn, height above the rings, which way it looks, field of view), a direction of the Sun round the planet that puts the shadow in frame, and an exposure, all from `variants.py`'s tiles. A glide moves every one of those numbers smoothly from one view to the next, so the shader draws the in-between frames as it draws the views. The exposure is eased in light, not in display values, so a glide doesn't flash.
  - **As built:** from the unlit face one exposure serves every view (1.478, as sheet 5 found). From the lit face each view has its own, 0.98 to 2.44 (step (c)): no one exposure held every view's 99.9th within 175–200, and a glide eases it by its logarithm, in light. A glide takes 120 s, eased at both ends (smoothstep), and moves the height by its logarithm. The distance from Saturn moves in step with the height rather than the time, so the camera crosses most of the ground while it's high, where the ground moves least across the screen, and where the ground to cross is far for the height (out of Daphnis's 120 km to any view but the Encke gap's), the camera climbs in the middle until the ground under it moves no more than 2% of its height a second at the glide's fastest. The yaw goes the short way round. The Sun goes round the side away from 180°: going through it, the planet's shadow swept over the camera's own patch of ring in a second or two, a flash.
- **The camera keeps station against the Sun.** **As built:** the Sun holds still relative to each view, where the plan had it turn at the camera's orbital rate. Over hours that would have put view A back on the planet's night side, the black disc the round-2 mock-ups moved its Sun to avoid. The Sun turns only in a glide, from one view's place for it to the next one's.
- **The drift while a view holds.** **As built:** the camera drifts out from Saturn and back once a hold, easing at both ends, so the bands slide slowly under it, the pace the films found calm (20 km/s at its fastest at 4 Mm up, 1–2 levels of 255 a frame), in proportion to the height so the near bands cross the screen at about the same pace in every view: 3.06 Mm in along the rings (views B and C), 1.22 Mm out over the Encke gap (E). Looking in (A) and from above (D) are held to 3 and 4 Mm, a few percent of the planet's distance: their pictures are of the planet, which at the same pace (60 and 200 km/s) would have swung out of frame. In Daphnis's view the camera drifts along the Keeler gap instead, so the moon comes nearer from 1.5 Mm ahead (a speck near the top) to 0.2 Mm (large, lower left) through one hold, and goes back through the next if the view is held. It stays in frame throughout: it never leaves the view empty, and the glide carries the camera away from it.
  - **As built (step (c)):** the drift runs on its own clock, which Speed runs and nothing else does: out and back in 8 minutes at 1× (Daphnis near in 8 and back in 8) whatever the Hold, so a short hold can't hurry it and the battery rates measured from it hold. A hold can end anywhere in its drift, so a glide sets out from the view still drifting, eased away, rather than from where the camera stood, which would stop it dead first. A hold and a glide keep their own lengths at any Speed.
- **Daphnis** is in the shader only in its own view: it and its waves fade in and out over the glides to and from it, while they are a few pixels across.
- **Fly Through.** **As built (2026-11-19, 0.130.14):** Dave asked for "a continuous flythrough based on one smooth path through all the views", as its own setting. The camera flies one closed loop through the six views, round and round, never slowing at a view (`SaturnsRings.path`, `flying`):
  - **The order:** Looking In, Along to the Horizon, Over the Encke Gap, Daphnis Close, Along the Planet's Edge, From Above, and back to Looking In. Of the 48 orders (from Looking In, either way round) that keep the two low views side by side, it is the shortest at the even pace, 1,767 s; the same loop the other way is 1,776 s, the next 1,827 (`saturnsRingsFlightOrders`, as measured again in 0.130.15). It comes down to Daphnis Close from the Encke gap, 1.6 Mm up, rather than from 4.
  - **The path:** a periodic cubic through the views' numbers (r, the height's logarithm, yaw, pitch, field, the Sun in the space that avoids 180°, the exposure's logarithm), so every number and its rate run on unbroken through every view, and the picture at a view is its tile. Its slope at a view is Catmull-Rom's (the mean of the slopes either side), but for the distance out, the height and the turn (0.130.15) level where the view is a peak or trough of that number and held to three times the gentler slope elsewhere (Fritsch and Carlson's limit), so those never overshoot: Catmull-Rom's own carried the camera past Daphnis Close and out beyond the A ring's edge, half the screen empty, before it came back in to the Encke gap. **As built (0.130.15):** at first every number was limited so; where every number is at a peak or level at once, as at Looking In just after a crossing, the camera then stopped dead, so the yaw, pitch, field, Sun and exposures take Catmull-Rom's own slope, which overshoots them a little, harmlessly, and keeps the camera moving. The climb `between` makes in a glide between two views is folded in as a bump of the height's logarithm that starts and ends level (sin²). Daphnis fades in over the stretch to its view and out over the one after (smoothstep), and comes from 2 Mm ahead to 0.6 at its view, as in its tile, and on past, so the moon passes in frame.
  - **The pace:** the loop is cut into 720 steps, and each takes the time the fastest thing on screen needs to cross it at 30 device px a second (`motion`, the same measure the battery rates rest on), averaged with the six steps either side (0.130.15: unaveraged, where the fastest thing changed from one to another, as the shadow's edge came into the frame, the camera's own speed changed five times over in a frame), so the pace on screen is even whatever the camera does: from 20.9 to 33.6 px/s all round at 1×, and the ground under the camera never crosses more than 1.6% of its height a second (`calm` is 2%). That makes the loop 1,767 s at 1×, 29 minutes, about 5 between views. Speed runs it: 0 stops it. The measure now samples the planet's shadow's two edges along their length: they sweep across the rings as the Sun turns, and a grid of rays caught them only now and then, so the first loop raced past them at up to 77 px/s.
  - **Switching** between Fly Through, Cycle and a view glides from wherever the camera is, as picking a view partway through a glide does: onto the loop where it passes the view the camera was at, arriving moving with it; off it from the loop still flying on; and to Cycle, off the loop to the next view it would have passed, to hold there.
- **Changing faces.** **As built (2026-11-20, 0.130.15):** the camera goes from one face to the other by flying through the ring plane where nothing is near: the crossing point (`SaturnsRings.crossing`), level with the plane 150 Mm out, Looking In's distance, past the A ring's edge (136.8 Mm) and the F ring (140.2), where the nearest ring is 8 Mm away and the rings are a hairline across the planet, the Cassini-equinox picture. It steps from 200 m on one side to 200 m on the other in one frame, never at 0 (the shader divides by the height), and the shader takes the other face's formula and colours there.
  - **The turn.** The lit views are the unlit ones mirrored, so the plane is below on the screen from either face; level with the plane, the two faces' cameras look the same way but turned half round from each other about it. So a camera changing faces turns a quarter on the way to the crossing point (the hairline stands up the screen there) and a quarter on the way from it, and the picture carries on through the change unbroken: either side of the step, its place and its axes agree to a ten-millionth.
  - **The hairline fades.** Out past the rings the shader fades them over the camera's last 20 km to the plane: seen edge on they are a sheet a fraction of a pixel thick, and a band of whole pixels stood for it, which jumped from one side of the horizon to the other as the camera went through. At 200 m it's down to a hundredth.
  - **The exposures** (the rings' and the planet's, now each the camera's own) ease in light across the move; at the crossing point they're halfway between Looking In's from the two faces.
  - **The ways it crosses:** a glide to the crossing point and one on from it, each `between`'s 120 s, eased at both ends, so the camera comes to rest at the crossing point a moment (a crossing move is 240 s); `between` moves the distance out in step with the height, so the camera is out past the rings while it's high and dives only beyond them. From a view held under Alternate every half hour, back to the same view; in Cycle under Alternate every other glide, on to the next view; under the appearance, or when Face is changed, from wherever the camera is, back to the view or the loop it was at or going to. Fly Through under Alternate flies a two-lap loop (`route(alternating:)`), lit then unlit, each lap ending out past the rings at Looking In's distance 2 Mm up (so the camera is beyond the rings before it comes down: without that knot it passed 100–150 km over the F ring's edge of the rings, which no grid of rays caught, and raced there) and then at the crossing point, as knots of the same spline at the same even pace; its turn counts on round the knots, a quarter at each crossing.
  - **Follow** reads the appearance as the other light and dark wallpapers do (`systemIsDark`), each frame, and the wallpaper is marked `followsAppearance`, so the app no longer rebuilds it when the appearance changes: the camera crosses instead. Building the scene picks the side at once: Alternate starts lit (on the two-lap loop's lit lap), Follow as the appearance is.
- **The light is today's:** the Sun's height over the ring plane from the date (Saturn's orbit is in `SkyMath`, its pole from the IAU), checked against JPL Horizons' 8.4° south for 2026-11-18. The unlit face is the one the Sun isn't on.
  - **As built (step (d)):** the Sun as seen from Saturn's centre by `SkyMath`'s orbit (JPL's approximate elements, `Sky.sun(from:)`), and the IAU's pole (Sources), give its height over the ring plane, read as the scene is built: it moves about 0.3° a month, and a scene is built at least every 3 days (the app's 12-hour rebuild when the displays sleep). It agrees with Horizons to 0.06° at nine dates from Cassini's 2009 equinox to 2039 (The build so far, (d)). Being fixed for a scene, it can't cross the plane mid-glide; it's kept 0.05° off the plane, more than the Sun's own radius there, as the shader divides by it. At the 2039 equinox the faces change sides, so the camera goes to the other side of the plane at the next build, the same views mirrored.
  - **As built:** the rings' exposure is lifted when the Sun is lower than the 8.4° the exposures were set for (`SaturnsRings.lift`), or near the equinox the views of rings alone would go all but black; the planet keeps its own.
- **Two faces, one shader.** **As built (step (c)):** Face puts the camera on the other side of the ring plane, the same views mirrored (the `side` sign in `Camera.axes`); the shader already draws either, by which side the camera and the Sun are on. The lit face takes its own colours (PIA06485's, as `mock.swift` has them) and its own exposure per view, and its planet takes the same exposure, with no cap.
- **The name's files:** the app makes a wallpaper's file names from its name, lowercased with hyphens for spaces, which would put an apostrophe in `preview-saturn's-rings.jpg`. One shared helper drops apostrophes for every caller (Settings' preview, the menu's loop, the trim shots), so the files are `preview-saturns-rings.jpg` and `menu-saturns-rings.mp4`, and the doc's slug matches.

### Build order
Each step a commit once it builds and the tests pass, with its snapshot looked at, behind `unfinished: true`, in a worktree on the branch `saturns-rings`.
- **(a) The still,** in view C, unlit face, today's Sun fixed at 8.4° south: the shader from `mock.swift`, the profile as a resource, the pyramid baked as the scene is built (measured, and shared between copies by `sharedTexture`), the slug helper, `preview-saturns-rings.jpg`, and the About credit for the ring data.
- **(b) The six views and the glide:** View (Cycle, or one of the six), the hold and the glide, the slow drift within a view, the Sun turning at the camera's real orbital rate, Daphnis on its orbit in its own view; `batteryRate` from the fastest motion.
- **(c) Settings:** Face (Unlit, Lit), Speed, Brightness, how long a view holds, Stars; a way to pin the view, the clock and the date for pictures.
- **(d) Live and real:** the Sun's height from the date; the real stars and Milky Way from Black Hole's catalogue and glow in place of random ones.

- **The sky.** **As built (step (d)):** Black Hole's catalogue (HYG's stars to magnitude 11, in a grid of 0.18° cells) and glow (ESO's Milky Way with its own stars taken out), shared through `sharedTexture`. Each view holds its Sun at its own place round the planet, so the sky is turned with it: a direction is taken to the frame with the Sun at azimuth 0 round the pole, then to the real sky by the pole and the Sun's real direction that day, then to the files' frame by `NightSky.toStars`. A star is a dot 0.9 points across, at luma 30 for the brightest (magnitude −1.5), falling as the square of Black Hole's brightness code (5 at magnitude 6, gone by 9), and the glow adds up to about 17 at the galactic centre; the two together never pass 30 over the black (the look report's cap for stars). Stars turns both off.

## Settings
- **View** (`saturn.view`, the first knob, a menu, in its own section): which view, Cycle or Fly Through. Stored as a number, for good once shipped: **0** Cycle (the standard), **1** Looking In, **2** Along to the Horizon, **3** Along the Planet's Edge, **4** From Above, **5** Over the Encke Gap, **6** Daphnis Close, **7** Fly Through (0.130.14; stored after the views so their numbers kept their meanings, listed second, after Cycle, by the knob's `order`).
- **Hold each view** (`saturn.hold`, minutes, 1 to 30, standard 8; it was 2 to 30 until Dave asked for a minute, 0.130.12): how long each view holds in Cycle, shown only while View is at Cycle. The glide is 120 s, not a setting.
- **Face** (`saturn.face`, a menu, in Look): **0** Unlit, **1** Lit (the standard since 0.130.13; it was Unlit before), **2** Alternate (0.130.15: changes faces on its own), **3** lit in Light Mode, unlit in Dark Mode (0.130.15). Listed Lit, Unlit, Alternate, then the fourth, by the knob's `order`.
- **Brightness** (`saturn.brightness`, 0.5 to 2, standard 1): the exposure (and the planet's), times the view's own.
- **Stars** (`saturn.stars`, a switch, standard on: **1** on, **0** off).
- **Speed** (`saturn.speed`, 0 to 10×, standard 1, in Motion): how fast the drift goes, and Daphnis comes near. 0 holds each view still.
- **For pictures** (not in Settings): `saturn.moment`, seconds into the first hold, read as the scene is built; the drift is that far on times Speed. With a view picked it pins the moment of the drift; in Cycle the first view is random. `saturn.date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`, a string) pins the day the light and the sky are worked out for; without it, it's the day the scene is built. Night Sky pins its time of day with a Settings row (Preview a time of day) and Black Hole has no date; this is a picture's pin, as `saturn.moment` is, so it stays out of Settings.

## Cost
Measured in a release build at 3024 × 1964 (2x Retina), by the render test with each view pinned, two runs each, and by `saturnsRingsStrip` timing 60 frames from a glide's middle the same way:
- **Drawing** (step (d), with the real sky): 0.50–0.66 ms of GPU a frame from the unlit face and 0.47–0.62 from the lit, the most in Daphnis Close, where the sky shows through the A ring over most of the frame and the stars are searched there; 0.56–0.58 mid-glide. CPU 0.36–0.47 ms a frame. The budget is about 2 of each. The sky added 0.1–0.2 ms of GPU to step (c)'s 0.34–0.51: it's worked out only where it shows through by more than 1/60 (less adds under half a level), and a star is looked for only in the cells it could reach, one or two each way. Step (a)'s still was 0.38–0.39.
- **Building, on the main thread** (`saturnsRingsBuildTime`, step (d)): the first copy builds in 50–53 ms, making the shared textures: reading the profile 10 ms, baking the pyramid 8.5, the star grid 5–6, the glow 8. That is over the 30 ms at which the plan was to say so: it stays on the main thread for now, as a scene must draw complete as it's built. Where the app hasn't yet read a HEIC since it started, the glow's first read starts the decoder, about 100 ms more (the first copy then 116–131 ms). A second copy, built while the first is showing, shares the textures: 8–9 ms. The first frame draws in 5 ms, or 14–16 the first time the shader is compiled.
- **Fly Through** (0.130.14): 0.61 ms of GPU and 0.42 of CPU a frame at the loop's fastest point (`saturnsRingsStrip`, `fly@890`), as a view's.
- **Changing faces** (0.130.15): 0.47 ms of GPU and 0.42 of CPU a frame through a held view's crossing, 0.40 and 0.38 through the two-lap loop's; the two-lap loop is worked out the first time it's flown, as the one-face loop is. Its loop is worked out the first time it's flown, 18 ms on the main thread in release, and kept (a few thousand numbers); each frame finds its place on it and works out the camera from the spline, as a glide does.
- **Memory:** the pyramid's 5.5 MB of texels, the star grid's 8 MB and the glow's 2 MB, and their copies on the GPU.
- **The shader:** 18 uniforms of the about 30 a shader can take (`u_size`, `u_table`, `u_cam`, `u_rot`, `u_sun`, `u_lens`, `u_moon`, `u_stars`, `u_catalogue`, `u_galaxy`, `u_sky`, the two exposures and five colours), and three textures. The camera's five and the exposures change every frame; the colours when Face does; the sky's turn as the scene is built.
- **On battery** (`BatteryPaced`, by the rule of about 2 device px a frame): while a view holds, the lowest of 10, 12, 15 and 20 fps that keeps its fastest thing, its `pace` (measured at 1×: A 3.0 device px/s, B 7.7, C 7.5, D 1.7, E 8.7, F 45.1, the waves passing the bottom of the screen) times Speed, to 2 px a frame or less, else none, for the battery's 30; none in a glide (51–176 px/s), whatever Speed; in Fly Through, the lowest that keeps its fastest, 34 px/s at 1× (`flightMost`, measured 33.6), times Speed to 2 px a frame or less: 20 fps at 1×, 10 at 0.5×, none above 1.17×; on the two-lap loop 39 (`alternateMost`, measured 37.5): 20 fps at 1×, 10 at 0.5×; none while the camera glides to or from the crossing point. The lit face moves the same, mirrored. `saturnsRingsMotionFitsTheBatteryRates` measures each pace and checks the rate at 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 10×, and that the next rate down would break the rule:

  | View | 0.5× | 1× | 2× | 4× | 10× |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Looking In | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 15 |
  | Along to the Horizon | 10 | 10 | 10 | 20 | 30 |
  | Along the Planet's Edge | 10 | 10 | 10 | 15 | 30 |
  | From Above | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
  | Over the Encke Gap | 10 | 10 | 10 | 20 | 30 |
  | Daphnis Close | 12 | 30 | 30 | 30 | 30 |

## Shortcuts
The `ponytail:` comments in `SaturnsRings.swift`, each with where it stops being good enough:
- **The rings' lift toward the equinox** is a law fitted by eye to the dates measured (1.6° and 0.1°), keeping the views of rings alone within about a fifth of today's 99th percentile, not the rings' real photometry: work it out from the slab's light if a date between looks too bright or too dark.
- **A glide's climb is worked out again every frame,** about 1,600 exponentials, microseconds, and so is the loop's climb while flying: keep it for the glide or the stretch of loop if the CPU a frame ever shows it.
- **A crossing pauses at the crossing point:** it's two glides, each eased at both ends, so the camera comes to rest there a moment between them; one move through it (the loop's spline from the view to the view) if the pause is ever seen as a stop.
- **Fly Through's pace is even by steps:** the time is linear within each of its 720 steps, so its speed changes by a few percent from one to the next, every 2 or 3 seconds (the strip at its fastest shows none of it): a smooth curve through the table if a step is ever seen.
- **The first copy builds on the main thread,** about 50 ms (the profile read, the pyramid's bake, the star grid and the glow), or about 150 ms while the HEIC decoder first starts: bake them off the main thread behind a still if a switch to the wallpaper is seen to stall. The profile is read on every build (10 ms), though the shader needs only its first radius, step and length: keep those beside the shared pyramid first.
- **The profile is 8 km a sample.** View C's bottom edge covers about 6 km of radius a device pixel, near enough. The close views draw finer near the bottom, E about 1.6 km a pixel and F about 0.1 km, so their near fields are the profile smoothed, as in the tiles Dave picked: a 4 km profile (16,384 texels, Metal's widest) if they look soft.
- **The stars' colours are two,** warm and cool by temperature, not Black Hole's black-body table (a fourth texture), as no star passes luma 30: the table if they're ever drawn bright enough for colour to show. **A star within 3° of the star file's poles is cut,** where its cells are too narrow to search: search further if one is seen.
- **Daphnis fades** with its waves over a glide into or out of its view, while the camera is a few hundred km to a few Mm off and they are a few pixels across: place it on its real orbit in every view if the fade ever shows.
- **The planet's bands are drawn by latitude,** not mapped: a Cassini map of the globe if a view close to it shows them as too regular.

## Still open
- **The planet's limb is a hard edge,** a pixel either planet or sky: as the camera moves, pixels grazing it flip, a few in a frame (measured at the crossing, 18 to 48 values between two frames with the camera all but still). Smoothing it over a pixel, as research-method.md §4 proposed, would steady it everywhere.
- **Motion, measured before step (b)** (2026-11-19, from films the mock drew and then deleted, since Dave didn't need them): from frame to frame at 30 fps the bands change by 1 level of 255 at the 99th percentile and 2 at the 99.9th at 1× (the camera drifting 20 km/s), and by 7 and 19 at 10×, with no crawling or popping in contact sheets of either. At 1× the bands creep 400 km in 20 s: calm, close to a still.
- **Daphnis hardly moves at its real speed:** 3.1 m/s against the Keeler gap's inner edge, so in its view at 1× the moon and its waves stand still; the film needed 8,000× to show it passing (with the Sun held still, since at that pace it would go round in 6 s). So in step (b) the passing has to come from the camera, not the moon: a camera a little off the moon's orbit sees it go by at the shear between them.
