# Loom

Cloth being woven: a historic coverlet's pattern growing pick by pick at the fell, near the top of the screen, while the finished cloth moves slowly down and away, so the screen is always full of cloth. The pattern comes from real weave drafts in public-domain pattern books, the colours from real dyes (indigo, madder, walnut, undyed wool), and the cloth is drawn thread by thread, a wool pattern weft floating over a fine cotton ground.

It was an idea on the "Atrium Wallpaper Ideas" page of 2026-11-14 (a review of the catalogue by the session atrium-37), not one of its five picks, in these words: "Cloth growing row by row, its pattern appearing as it is woven, then rolling on to the next." What is real: "Historic weave drafts from public-domain pattern books, in indigo and madder families." Builds on: "Peg Board's patience and order of building, with thread shaded to read as soft cloth." Risk, medium: "A fine regular weave can shimmer on screen, as Peg Board's sheet did when reduced." Dave ran `/new-wallpaper` on it on 2026-11-18, adding: "just note we cut pegboard and have no plans to bring it back but you can use its code as reference". That day two agents researched it and one round of mock-ups was drawn. Nothing is built.

- **Source:** none yet.
- **Data:** none yet. The mock-ups' drafts are Mary Meigs Atwater's (1928), transcribed from her text ([The drafts](#the-drafts)).
- **Research:** [the method](loom/research-method.md) (drafts and drawdowns, the real numbers of coverlets and hand weaving, the view and the motion, shimmer measured, cost measured with a SpriteKit probe) and [the look](loom/research-look.md) (real coverlets and references, palettes sampled from museum pieces, brightness and busyness measured, the draft books and their rights, the names). Every figure in them is marked checked or unchecked.
- **Scripts:** `loom/mock.swift` draws the mock-ups with SpriteKit as the app would; `sheet.py` lays out the sheets and films from the variants in `variants.py`.
- **Where it stands (2026-11-18):** round 1 of mock-ups waits for Dave's picks.

## Names and look-alikes
- **Loom** is the name on the ideas page. It is an ordinary word, but it is also Atlassian's screen recorder, which runs on Macs ([research-look.md](loom/research-look.md) §6), in exactly the place Atrium lives. **Weaving** is plain, as Etching is, names what happens on screen, and no app or mark of that name turned up; **Coverlet** names the cloth but is wrong for a twill. Handloom is a certification mark in India: out.
- **Not a toy, not Peg Board.** Peg Board was cut for being a toy's picture, bright mixed colours on a sheet nearly empty most of each round, and the same act as Etching (a picture built from nothing, held, cleared). Here nothing is cleared and nothing is held: the cloth is endless, the screen always full, and one piece runs on into the next on the same warp, as weavers wove them.
- **Traditions not used as patterns:** Diné (Navajo) weaving, kente, ritual ikats, tartans ([research-look.md](loom/research-look.md) §6). Pattern names are screened: many are religious (Rose of Sharon, Church Windows) or political (Whig Rose, Lee's Surrender), so only a screened list may appear anywhere in the app.

## Mock-ups, round 1 (2026-11-18)
Drawn by `mock.swift` through SKRenderer, as the render test draws a wallpaper. The cloth is one shader reading a drawdown baked from a real draft, a texel a crossing (which thread is on top, its colour, where its float begins and ends), the method research's design. Every end and row is a whole number of device pixels (the method's rule against shimmer). What the look research says makes cloth read as cloth is in the shader: round threads, wool floats that bulge and are hairy at their edges and lie in shadow where they dive, a fine cotton ground, and a little unevenness in every thread. A pattern pick and its tabby are about two ends tall, as in real coverlets (30 ends and 13–18 pattern picks an inch), so overshot is woven "until square". Desktop tiles are a 14-inch display at 2x averaged down; close-ups are 1:1 device pixels. Each tile carries the mock's own GPU and CPU time, its brightness (the brightest of R, G and B: median and 99th percentile at desktop size) and its busyness (the look research's measure).

```sh
cd docs/loom && python3 sheet.py fetch   # the reference coverlet, CC0, into its own folder under $TMPDIR
python3 sheet.py sheet1-kind .           # or sheet2-close … sheet7-desk, film1-standard, film2-weaver
```

Each question, with my recommendation:

1. **Which kinds of cloth** ([sheet1-kind.jpg](loom/sheet1-kind.jpg)): A overshot (Honeysuckle), B summer and winter, C double weave, and D a twill (Goose-Eye). B and C are Honeysuckle's own blocks, arranged for those weaves as Atwater describes, so they are the same figure at four times the size. **Recommended:** overshot and summer and winter taking turns, a new cloth each time, with a Kind menu to keep to one. Double weave reads as blocks of flat colour, nearest a pixel picture (Peg Board's ground); the twill is a fine texture with no figure to watch grow, better as the plain heading between pieces.
2. **Does it read as cloth?** ([sheet2-close.jpg](loom/sheet2-close.jpg)): a real overshot coverlet of about 1840 at life size (A, the Art Institute of Chicago's 66024, CC0) beside the mock's soft look (B) and crisp look (C) in the same colours, and summer and winter, double weave and the twill close up. **Recommended:** the soft look. It is nearer, not there: the real wool is fatter and hairier, its pillows deeper.
3. **How big a thread** ([sheet3-size.jpg](loom/sheet3-size.jpg)): 3, 5, 8 and 12 device pixels an end. 8 is life size on a 14-inch screen. **Recommended:** life size as standard and a Thread size setting from 6 to 12. Below 6 the method research measured the Settings preview's copy banding, and the pattern turns to texture (A's busyness is 56%).
4. **The colours, Light Mode** ([sheet4-colour.jpg](loom/sheet4-colour.jpg)): Indigo held to the look research's caps (A), Indigo at a real coverlet's navy-on-white contrast (B), Madder (C), Walnut (D), Undyed (E), and Red and Blue (F), the classic coverlet but two colour families. **Recommended:** Indigo as standard, Madder, Walnut and Undyed as picks, B as a Contrast slider's top rather than a palette, and F left out, as Lava Lamp's green-in-blue was.
5. **The colours, Dark Mode** ([sheet5-dark.jpg](loom/sheet5-dark.jpg)): Blue on Blue (A, after the Met's double weave of 1836 in two indigos), Madder Dark (B), Walnut Dark (C), Black Sheep (D). **Recommended:** follow the system's appearance, Blue on Blue with Indigo and Madder Dark with Madder; Walnut Dark and Black Sheep only if he likes them, since browns go muddy dimmed.
6. **The view** ([sheet6-view.jpg](loom/sheet6-view.jpg)): A cloth to the edges, a finger of open warp at the top; B a wider band of warp under the reed; C the cloth's selvedges with the floor beside it; D the loom from the weaver's bench, in perspective. **Recommended:** A, the method research's view: the most cloth and nothing drawn but cloth. D is the most "loom" but a drawn machine is what sank A Tree for the Year, and its far cloth already shimmers in the mock.
7. **The motion** ([film1-standard.mp4](loom/film1-standard.mp4), [film2-weaver.mp4](loom/film2-weaver.mp4)): 12 s of each. The first is the proposed standard pace, half a weaver's (a pattern pick and its tabby every 10 s, the cloth moving down 1.6 px a second in whole pixels, a screen in about 20 minutes), the shuttle's soft front crossing in a second and a half. The second is a weaver's own pace sped up six times so the cloth is seen wound on: the fell climbs a hand's width and then the cloth is wound down 5 cm over four seconds, as on a real loom. **Recommended:** the standard pace, steady, with a Speed slider from a strip a day to a weaver's pace and beyond for a demo. Winding on in steps is truer but the one big motion in an otherwise still picture.
8. **The name:** Loom, Weaving or Coverlet. **Recommended:** Weaving.

[sheet7-desk.jpg](loom/sheet7-desk.jpg) is overshot in indigo at life size, at a 14-inch display's size under a menu bar and desktop folders.

## What the mock-ups showed
- **The drafts had to be read from text.** Atwater's drafts are printed as grids of black squares, and the scans' ink fills cells and runs into the lines: a reader of the grids gave a threading for Honeysuckle that disagreed with her own heddle count and treadling. Her text spells out the first 16 ends, and the count and treadling fix the rest, block for block. So the mock-ups have two drafts that are certainly hers, Honeysuckle (No. 6) and Goose-Eye (No. 291), and Honeysuckle's blocks rewoven as summer and winter and double weave. A build needs a dozen more, each counted by hand from a scan and checked, the method research's estimate of a few hours a draft.
- **Busy, in Light Mode.** The look research's measure counts every step of brightness over 0.08 between neighbouring pixels at 1,920 px across. The cloth scores 31–56% in Light Mode (Etching's light paper is 16.9%; the wallpapers Dave keeps score 0–3%; Game of Life's Classic, "so crazy on the eyes", 11.1%), from the thread texture as much as the pattern: life-size threads at 1,920 px are 5 px, and each has a lit crown and a shadowed edge. Dark Mode scores 1–12%. A Light Mode build should keep neighbouring threads within about 20 levels of each other, as the look research proposes, and Dave should judge it at full size on his screen, not in a tile.
- **Brightness held to the caps.** Light Mode: median 93–151, 99th percentile 207–223 (caps 150–190 and 230). Dark Mode: median 46–59, 99th 83–108 (caps 25–45 and 120); a touch over at the median, since Honeysuckle is mostly wool.
- **The mock's own cost** is about 2 ms of GPU at 3024 × 1964, for the wool's three reads, its noise and its hair, unoptimised; the double weave and the twill, without wool, 0.5 ms. The method research's lean probe drew a thread shader in 0.28–0.30 ms. A build has to get the wool's look within budget: likely the hair and strands baked into a small texture rather than worked out by noise per pixel.
- **The real wool is fatter.** Beside the Art Institute's coverlet the mock's floats are thinner and smoother, and its ground more open. That piece's wool pattern pick and its tabby are three ends tall; Atwater's yarns square her treadling at about one. The mock's two-ends standard sits between.
- **Not drawn yet:** the beat (the new pick settling into the fell), a shuttle, the heading between pieces, and the settings preview at its own scale.
