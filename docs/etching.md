# Etching

Old etchings, engravings and woodcuts drawn line by line: each one slowly, in one unbroken line, on paper, on parchment, on a blueprint's sheet or on a screen of grey powder. The line is the Etch A Sketch's, drawn by a stylus that can never lift. The Powder surface is that toy's screen as well: the stylus scrapes the powder off the back of the glass, so the line is the dark inside showing through. That name is used nowhere in the app.

**Shown to everyone from 0.111.0** (2026-10-13, when Dave said "unhide all 3 of the new ones"; it was built behind the `unfinished` switch). **From 0.112.0 it is drawn on paper** unless another surface is picked, and its screen can follow day and night ([Dave's decisions](#daves-decisions-2026-10-12)). He asked for it on 2026-10-12 (a wallpaper in that toy's style "where intricate public domain images become slowly drawn out over time starting from fresh"), it was researched and mocked up that day, and built the same day in three steps.

**The name is Dave's pick** (2026-10-12): Etching names the old prints being drawn, is not a piece of the toy's mark, and nobody holds it ([below](#names-and-look-alikes)). It was built under the working name "Line Drawing", which is why its module is still `LineRoute`.

## Names and look-alikes

From the rights research of 2026-10-12, which read the US Patent and Trademark Office's records (status pages are `https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn<serial>`). Not a lawyer's advice.

- **The toy's name is a registered word mark** of Spin Master Ltd. (reg. 725595, serial 72110281, live), and since 2026-05-05 also for "downloadable computer software applications featuring digital art and drawing" (reg. 8237930, serial 97223677). So the name is not the wallpaper's, and appears in no menu, setting, store listing or keyword.
- **The toy's shape is registered too** (reg. 1587707, serial 73701288): a drawing of a frame, a screen and two circles in the lower corners, with no colour claimed. Red with white knobs is its famous look. So a frame, if one comes, is plainly not the toy's: another material and colour, the dials not in the lower corners.
- **The mechanism is free:** one unbroken line scraped into grey powder. The patents are long expired.
- **Dave asked whether "Etch" alone was safe.** Spin Master holds only the full name and two variants, and ten other owners hold ETCH for other things. It is an ordinary word, but it is the first word of the mark, and beside a grey screen and a dial-drawn line it reads as a reference to the toy. The research's order of risk, low to high: Line Drawing, Etching, Etch, the toy's full name. **Etching** names the pictures, is not a fragment of the mark, and no live mark is exactly ETCHING; Dave chose it.

## Dave's decisions (2026-10-12)

- **The line is unbroken, with shading:** the picture's outlines, then its shading as hatching, the stylus going back along lines already drawn to get from place to place. He saw it beside a line that lifts and chose this.
- **"Screen alone now, frame later."** The screen fills the display. He saw a walnut frame with brass dials and a graphite one mocked up, and chose "our own frame" for later; the picture's rectangle is kept relative to the screen so a frame can take a smaller one.
- **Detail: "Both, as a setting":** the toy's own line width, and a line half as wide. He will judge the default on his desktop; it starts at the toy's.
- **A Speed slider:** "be sure to include a speed slider on the line drawing (etch) wallpaper in case a user wants to see it go faster".
- **The screen follows the system's appearance:** 152 of 255 in Light Mode, 104 in Dark Mode. Changed the same day, once he saw it on his desktop, in Dark Mode: "something off with final etching background color, it does not match any of the mockups that showed a brighter paper like color, is it auto reacting to light and dark theme? it so we need to make the background on etching a configurable option". It was reacting: the mock-ups he approved all showed the bright screen, and his Mac showed the dim one. So the screen is now a setting, Screen: Light (the bright powder, the default, whatever the system is in), Dark, or Match System, which is what it did before. Once that reached his desktop: "the light screen looks right now" (2026-10-12). Light stays the default.
- **Pictures:** "nothing religious". Classical myth is fine.
- **On his desktop, the same evening:** "etching is great", and more drawings are wanted as options later ([the research note](#what-is-wrong-and-next)).
- **The picture fills the screen:** "lets also attempt to scale etching images up to "cover mode" so they fill the screen even if we loose a bit of content, or just make this an option in the settings, I personally would enable this if it was a setting". It is a setting, Fill the screen, and on unless turned off ([below](#filling-the-screen)).
- **A Screen pick that follows day and night** (2026-10-13): "lets also add an option in the etching settings under "screen" for a "follow day/night" and auto change light to dark based on time of day, add that as a new option in addition to what is currently there". It is the Screen menu's fourth pick, Follow Day and Night, after the three that were there, and Light stays the default. That it goes by the real Sun where the Mac is, and takes the hour of dusk over it, is the lead's call ([How it works](#how-it-works)).
- **A better surface to draw on** (2026-10-13): "lets try to make the background we draw upon a bit higher quality, maybe more paper like, just something that looks a bit sharper and cleaner". What was wrong, seen at 1:1 on his 14-inch screen: the powder was a soft cloudy mottle, blobs 20 to 40 pixels across, with bright specks, on a mid-grey ground under a grey line whose own tone the grain broke up. He was shown five surfaces as real renders, each in a light tone and a dark one (the powder as it was, the powder with a clean tooth, Paper, Parchment and Blueprint), and picked three things:
  - **All four new ones are offered:** Paper, Parchment, Blueprint, and the powder as cleaned. They are the Surface menu.
  - **Paper is what Etching starts on for everyone,** those who have run it before too. Their Screen pick carries over.
  - **Of the cloudy powder: "Replace it".** The cleaned one takes its place, as Powder, and nobody can get the cloudy one back.
  - The colours are the sheets', which he approved as shown ([the table](#the-surfaces)).
- **A finished picture is held for a time the user picks** (2026-10-13, of this wallpaper and Peg Board together): "do a 30 second hold at the end for both peg board and etching then move on (or just add an option to the settings panel on both to let user decide, default to 30s)". It is the option, worded the same on both: Hold when finished, 10 seconds, 30 seconds, 1 minute or 5 minutes, and 30 seconds unless changed. Before it a picture rested five minutes at speed 1 and less when hurried, never under 20 s.

The lead's calls, which Dave has not ruled on: progress follows the wall clock; a finished picture rests, clears softly to a ghost, and the next begins; the gone-over line is darker; the Picture menu has Random first; a change of Surface eases over two seconds as a change of Screen does; the line crosses from one tone's colour to the other's by itself, half-way through a change of tone, so that it reads all through dusk; and the dark tones have more tooth than the sheets he picked from, where they came out flat.

## How it works

- **A picture is a route**, not an image: the cells the stylus steps through, in order, on a grid of 1024 × 640 (one cell is one step; the line is 1.44 cells wide). `docs/etching/bake.py` makes a route from a picture; the app ships only routes (`Resources/etching-<name>.route`).
- **The route is stamped once** into two textures, off the main thread (`LineRoute.stamp`, in the `LineRoute` module, which is built optimised even in debug). One holds when each texel is first drawn, as a 24-bit share of the route's clock, and how much of it the finished line covers; the other when it is gone over again, to the same 24 bits, and the ghost of the picture before. Until the stamp lands the screen is bare, which is how a drawing starts anyway; then the picture so far fades up over 0.4 s.
- **One shader compares those times with a clock.** So a frame costs the same however much is drawn, and the CPU does nothing per frame but set the clock.
- **Every read of a texture is at a texel's centre.** SpriteKit smooths any texture a shader reads, whatever its filtering mode says, and a time smoothed between a drawn texel and a bare one is a moment when nothing happened: lines would arrive thin and ragged. With a texel to each pixel that is one read of each texture. Above the 14-inch size the textures have a texel to two pixels each way, and the shader reads the four round each pixel, compares each with the clock, and only then mixes them: a pixel softer, a quarter of the memory. A standalone test before the build found 8.8% of pixels wrong when read at plain coordinates, with `.nearest` or `.linear` alike, and 24 in 5.9 million at texel centres.
- **The clock is the wall's** (`Etching.Cycle`). Which picture is on the screen, the one before it and the moment this one was begun are kept in the defaults (`etching.drawing`, `etching.before`, `etching.began`) and shared by every copy of the scene, so a relaunch, Shuffle or a second display goes on from the same place, and a picture carries on while the wallpaper is covered or the Mac is asleep. Moving the Speed slider, or changing the Detail, moves that moment, so the picture stays as far along as it was. Hold when finished moves it only for a picture that is past resting (two points down).
- **A picture is drawn, rests, clears, and the next begins.** It rests for the time picked in Hold when finished, 30 seconds unless changed and the same at any speed, then clears over 12 s whatever the speed: a soft fade, sooner in some places than others, as a shake lays the powder back unevenly. A ghost of its whole line stays (8% of the way to the dark inside) and fades away under the next picture, over its first six minutes of drawing at speed 1 (`Cycle.ghosting`). As first built the ghost stayed until the next clearing, and a print with big darks left a grey shape under the next picture for its whole time: a flaw, not a feature. The next picture is chosen as the clearing starts and stamped during it, so it begins on the frame the clearing ends.
- **Moving Hold when finished never changes the picture on the screen,** nor how far it is drawn, nor drops the screen into the middle of a clearing. The cycle keeps the rest it was last worked out for (`Cycle.rested`) beside the moment the picture was begun, and a new pick moves that moment only where it has to, as the Speed slider and a picture picked by name do. While a picture is being drawn nothing moves: the new time is simply the one it will rest. While it rests, a longer pick, or a shorter one it has not yet rested, leaves it resting until that time is up, counted from when it was finished; a shorter one it has already rested puts the moment where the rest ends now, so the clearing starts from its beginning there and then. While it clears, the moment moves by the difference between the two rests, so the clearing goes on from where it is.
- **A line drawn once is pale; gone over, it is the line's own tone.** The first pass draws a line 62% of the way from the ground to that tone (on Powder, the dark inside) and the second the rest, so outlines the stylus travels along come out bolder, as on the toy.
- **Going back along a line, the stylus goes eight times as fast** as it draws (`LineRoute.haste`, and `HASTE` in the bake). Nothing new appears while it does, but for a pale line turning dark the first time it is gone over. At twice the drawing pace, as first built, a picture stood still for up to 26 s at a time and for 32 to 39% of its clock; at eight times the longest spell is 3.6 to 6.5 s and it is still for 11 to 14%. The lead asked for this once Dave was to watch it at 1×; a frame with dials that keep turning would be the other answer.
- **The picture is drawn on one of four surfaces** (Surface: Paper, Parchment, Blueprint or Powder), each a row of `Etching.surfaces`: its bare ground and its line gone over, in a light tone and a dark one, and the ground's tooth in each ([The surfaces](#the-surfaces)). On Paper, Parchment and Blueprint the dark tone's line is lighter than its ground: chalk on charcoal paper, and a blueprint's white line.
- **Which tone is a setting** (Screen: Light, Dark, Match System or Follow Day and Night), read every frame. The scene keeps one number, `shade`, 0 for the light tone and 1 for the dark one, which goes toward the one the setting asks for at half a second⁻¹, and `tint` eases it into the ground's colour and its tooth. So changing the pick, or the system's appearance changing under Match System, takes the ground to the other tone over two seconds, under the picture, with nothing rebuilt: the scene watches the appearance itself (`followsAppearance`), so the app never builds it afresh for one.
- **The line crosses by itself** (`inked`). On three surfaces the line is darker than the ground in one tone and lighter in the other, so a line whose colour went by the shade would be the ground's own colour, and the picture gone, part of the way across (on Paper at 54%). That is a blink in a two-second change, but under Follow Day and Night it was about a quarter of an hour at dusk and again at dawn. So the line's colour has its own ease, at the same two-second rate: it heads for the dark tone's line once the shade is past a half, and for the light tone's short of it. A scene built at some shade starts with the line where it belongs. With the Sun half a degree up and half a degree down the line gone over is 77 to 119 levels from the ground on every surface, the mean of red, green and blue (the test asks for 60). Every surface goes the same way; on Powder both lines are dark, so nothing shows. What is left is the crossing itself: for part of those two seconds, as the Sun passes the horizon or half-way through a change of Screen, the line is the ground's colour.
- **A change of Surface eases too,** over the same two seconds, with nothing rebuilt and the picture untouched. The scene keeps the surface it is showing (`shown`), which is the picked one but for those two seconds: then it is part of the way there (`turned`) from whatever was on the screen when the pick changed, every colour and the tooth's weights mixed alike, so picking a third surface in the middle of a change goes on from where the screen is. Settings' live copy is a second scene reading the same setting, so it changes with the desktop.
- **Follow Day and Night goes by the real Sun where the Mac is,** not by the clock: the light tone by day, the dark one by night. The Sun's altitude is worked out as Flowing Gradient's is (`Sky.horizonMatrix` × `Sky.sun` at `Location.shared.coordinate`), once every 30 s, in which it climbs or sinks an eighth of a degree at the most. The shade it asks for can be anywhere from 0 to 1: 0 with the Sun 6° or more above the horizon, 1 with it 6° or more below, and a smoothstep between (`Etching.nightfall` is the straight ramp the shade follows, which `tint` then eases), so dusk and dawn take about an hour each at middle latitudes and nothing steps. The scene asks where the Mac is only while this is the pick, in `didMove(to:)` or on the first look after the pick is changed to it, because the first time macOS asks leave. Without leave the place is `Location`'s guess from the time zone, which is good enough to tell day from night. Not seen on a desktop through a real dusk: what was looked at is the tests' levels.
- **The picture fills the screen** unless Fill the screen is turned off: its own rectangle on the grid is scaled until it covers the display, and what is over is cut off at the edges ([below](#filling-the-screen)). Turned off, the whole grid is fitted below the top 4% of the screen, clear of the menu bar (a notched MacBook's bar is 3.8%), and centred, with bare ground beside it.
- **The ground has a tooth,** the same recipe on every surface, at the display's own pixels whatever their size, so nothing in it is ever magnified (a 5K or 6K display's too, where the line's textures have a texel to two pixels):
  - **a pixel's own noise,** from a hash of its place (`hash42`), 0.9% of the ground's level at one standard deviation;
  - **a soft tooth** two or three pixels across, 0.8%: `LineRoute.grain()`'s tile, 512 texels square, read a texel to a pixel at texel centres, twice;
  - **sparse fibres,** a pixel wide and 6 to 14 long, lying any way, 2.5% lighter or darker: one to a cell of each of two lattices, 53 and 61 pixels, which comes to one for every 40-pixel square;
  - **and nothing larger** but the light from the upper left, 2% brighter there and 2% darker at the lower right than the middle.
  Parchment's is a touch stronger (1.1%, 1.0% and 3.2%).
- **The dark tones have more tooth, as a share:** 2.0%, 2.4% and 5% (Powder 1.3%, 1.5% and 3.5%), which is 1.8 to 2.2 levels of green at one deviation where the light tones are 1.9 to 3.0. At the light tones' shares a dark ground came out within a level either way and read as flat colour; a bare Paper Dark screen had too few colours for the render test to pass it. At 2.3 levels it looked sandy.
- **A line darker than the ground takes the tooth in its own measure,** as ink does the paper's, so its tone is even to about 1.2% at one deviation; one lighter than the ground takes no more levels of it than the ground has (`tone + tooth × min(tone, ground)` in the shader). Before, the grain broke the line's tone up by a third and more.
- **Nothing in the ground repeats.** The noise and the fibres are hashes of where they are. The tile would repeat every 512 pixels, so each cell of the two fibre lattices reads it from another part of it, picked by that cell's hash: a bare screen is no more like itself 509, 512 or 1,024 pixels along than chance (0.00, where one pixel is 0.37 like the next and 0.09 like the one three along). The seams between cells are in a texture 2.5 pixels across and can't be found, looking at a bare screen with its contrast stretched eight times.
- **How clean:** on a bare Paper screen, with the light taken off, the level's spread is 1.22% of the level, 0.06% after a 16-pixel blur and 0.013% after an 80-pixel one. The cloudy powder's were 1.84%, 1.2% and 0.92%. The dark tones' are 3.2% and 0.17% (Powder 2.0% and 0.11%).
- **What the cloudy powder was,** for the record: a 512-texel tile of grain and soft mottle, magnified 1.18 times on a 14-inch screen; a slow unevenness, the same tile's other channel stretched six times over the screen; bright specks; and light falling off by 5% each way. The clearing still goes by that slow unevenness, which is a pattern in time and never seen at rest; the rest is gone from the shader and from the tile.
- **Tried for the tooth and dropped:** the soft tooth as four hashes a pixel (0.42 ms of GPU a frame where the tile is 0.31); the tile read plainly, which repeats (a pixel 0.26 like the one 512 along); a second read turned on its side, which lines up with the first along diagonals and showed as a twill; fibres worked out for every pixel (0.33 ms where skipping the pixels far from one is 0.28).

## Filling the screen

Fill the screen (`etching.fill`, on unless turned off) is where a route's grid goes in the textures, and nothing else (`Etching.placement`). The picture's rectangle, which every route's heading gives, is scaled until it covers the display and centred; what is over lies outside the textures. The menu bar's band is not kept clear: the picture runs under it. The frame line every route begins with is six cells outside the picture, so it is never seen. **One picture is never filled:** the Airship, whose hull would lose both its rounded ends at a 14-inch screen's sides and read as a long box. It is marked whole or not at all (`Etching.whole`) and is fitted inside, below the menu bar, whichever way the switch is; no other picture is marked. Turning it on or off stamps the picture again, as changing the Detail does: the old textures stay up until the new ones land.

- **Nothing waits for a stylus that is out of sight.** In `LineRoute.stamp`, a step whose own line is off the textures' edge takes no time, and the clock it would have taken comes off every time after it. Tried first at the pace of going back along a line, eight times the drawing's: the frame alone held a bare screen for 15 s at the start of every picture. At 64 times it was 1.8 s, more than some pictures ever stand still fitted. A step is out of sight by its own line and not by its width: a row of hatching just off the edge was otherwise waited for, to show a sliver (11.5 s on the fine flea).
- **Under the menu bar is out of sight too.** The bar does not show the wallpaper through it, so a step whose line lies wholly in the top 4% of a filled screen (`placement`'s `covered`) takes no time either. Before it counted, the stylus worked there with nothing else changing for up to 83 s on Mishima Pass (133 fine), whose sky is a band of hatching along the top, 30 s on the flea (43) and 13 s on the Rhinoceros. The line is stamped there all the same, for a display that has no bar.
- **The shared cycle is as it was.** Which picture is up, and when it rests, clears and gives way, still come from the routes' own clocks and the wall's, the same for every display. A display whose picture is cut off finishes drawing before that clock says and rests the longer. So two displays of different shapes are a little differently far along the same route, and turning Fill on or off moves the picture a little along it.
- **The line is wider and the stylus quicker across the glass** by as much as the picture is scaled up: on a 14-inch screen from 1.13 times (Kajikazawa, Polyhedra, Twelve-Pointed Stars) to 1.44 (the Interlace), where the line is 1/494 of the screen's width for the fitted 1/711.
- **Going over a line again** is told from drawing it by the clock between the two passes (`apart`), and that clock is now the display's own. Stamped for a 14-inch screen with and without the time taken off, at most 2,200 of a picture's 0.4 to 2.2 million texels of line differ (the first six pictures) in whether they were gone over.

The photos: a classic toy with an outline drawing, the best unprocessed one found (https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5a/Etch_A_Sketch_engine_drawing.jpg, CC BY 2.0 per Wikimedia Commons); a close scan and whole works by George Vlosich III (https://gvartwork.com/pages/etch-a-sketch, no licence, reference only); three of Jane Labowitch's (https://www.intrepidtravel.com/adventures/the-worlds-best-etch-a-sketch-artist-does-india/, no licence, reference only).

- **How the experts shade** is what the routes copy: horizontal hatching at an even pitch, then vertical in the darks, then scraped solid, with thick outlines from going over them. Labowitch: "I sometimes have to go over the same area over a dozen times to get the darkest tones" (the page above).
- **How the toy works:** "The inside surface of the glass screen is coated with aluminum powder, which is then scraped off by a movable stylus"; "The "black" line merely exposes the darkness inside the toy" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etch_A_Sketch).
- **Not matched:** the real screen has a sheen that moves across it with the light, and its lines are softer and more often doubled, where going back along one misses by a line's width. No sharp unprocessed close-up of a single line was found, and no photo of a cleared screen: the clearing and its ghost are a guess.

## The route's file

Little-endian. `LRT1`; the grid's width and height in cells; the picture's rectangle on it (x, y, width, height); the start cell (x, y), all 16-bit; the number of steps (32-bit); the route's clock as a 32-bit float (cells of drawing, a step back along a line counting an eighth); the length of what follows (32-bit). Then the runs, deflated (raw DEFLATE, which `NSData.decompressed(using: .zlib)` reads): one byte to a run of steps one way, bit 7 set while the stylus goes back along its own line, bits 6 to 4 the direction (east, south, west, north, then the four diagonals from south-east round), bits 3 to 0 the run's length less one.

The app eases each position by one step (a quarter of each neighbour), so corners round a little, and takes the clock along the eased line.

## Making a route

```sh
python3 docs/etching/bake.py <folder of source pictures> Sources/Atrium/Resources          # every picture, standard
python3 docs/etching/bake.py <folder of source pictures> Sources/Atrium/Resources --fine   # every picture, fine
python3 docs/etching/bake.py <folder of source pictures> Sources/Atrium/Resources wave     # one
```

Needs numpy, scipy, Pillow and clang (for `route.c`, the one slow loop). The source pictures are not in the repo: `PICTURES` in `bake.py` gives each one's address, the file name it is looked for under, and its crop, with what else it needed: a sheet turned on its side (`rot`), signatures painted out (`blank`), a plate's faint construction lines dropped (`lift`), or a stained sheet's own tone taken out (`flatten`, below). A patent's file is one sheet of the Patent Office's PDF, taken out with `pdfimages` as `bake.py` says. A picture named in `FINE_ONLY` gets no standard route. A bake is the same every time. What it does:

1. **Darkness on the grid:** the picture, cropped, fitted to the grid with a margin, its paper tone taken as bare screen.
2. **Lines:** cells darker than their surroundings (the picture less a 2.5-cell blur of itself), thinned to one cell wide with Zhang and Suen's thinning (T. Y. Zhang and C. Y. Suen, "A fast parallel algorithm for thinning digital patterns", Communications of the ACM 27(3), 1984, https://doi.org/10.1145/357994.358023). Bold strokes are drawn first.
3. **Shading:** six layers switched on by darkness. Horizontal rows every 8 cells, then 4, then 2; vertical columns every 4, then 2; then solid. Neighbouring rows are joined at alternate ends, so the stylus zigzags as a hand does.
4. **One route:** the stylus walks to an undrawn neighbour, straight on first. Stuck, it takes the cheapest way to the nearest wanted cell: back along lines already drawn, or, where a piece of the picture stands alone, across bare screen (a stray mark, costed at twenty times a step back so it is the last resort). The whole picture's outlines come first, then each depth of shading over the whole picture, so a half-drawn picture is a whole composition and not a finished corner.

The fine routes are the same bake on a grid of 2048 × 1280, so everything is half the size: a line half as wide, hatching twice as close.

**`flatten`** (2026-10-13) is for a drawing on old paper, or a painted leaf, where no one tone is the bare sheet: a manuscript's page is darker at its edges and stained, and step 1 would draw the stains. Before anything else the whole sheet is turned into paper and ink (`flattened` in `bake.py`):

- **`flatten="paper"`:** the paper's tone is taken from the sheet itself, channel by channel: the 85th percentile of each neighbourhood 4% of the sheet's width across, worked out on a copy an eighth the size and blurred by half that width. Each channel is divided by it and the three are averaged, so stains and dark edges come out as bare paper.
- **`flatten="through"`:** the same, and the other side's writing taken out as well. Measured on scans of Leonardo's Codex Madrid I, what shows through the sheet is 14 to 19% darker than the paper in red but only 6 to 12% in green, where the ink is 50 to 80% darker in both. So twice the green less the red keeps the ink and cancels what shows through.

The crop, `lift` and `blank` then apply as for any picture. With no `flatten` the bake is as it was: fourteen pictures baked with and without the option in the script gave the same bytes.

Tried in the research and dropped: a line that lifts (floating dashes half-way, and two dials could not draw it), finishing the nearest ink first (a finished corner and a bare screen for an hour), hatching alone (stray marks across the bare areas), TSP art and a raster squiggle (neither looks like the toy or like a person drawing).

## Cost

Measured by the render test on a 14-inch screen at 2x (M3 Max, 2026-10-13): **0.44 to 0.57 ms of CPU and 0.30 to 0.36 ms of GPU a frame**, over 24 runs with 0.32 in the middle, on every surface and in either tone (one shader draws them all), bare, part drawn and while clearing. Before the surfaces it was 0.22 to 0.29 ms of GPU: the tooth is a tenth of a millisecond.

- **Building:** a standard route decodes in 2 ms and stamps in about 120 ms for 3024 × 1964, off the main thread; a fine one, of four times the steps, in about 150 ms. Filling the screen, where the line is wider, 49 to 161 ms, and 56 to 270 ms fine (fitted, 38 to 128 and 50 to 259). A scene built part-way through a picture stamps the one before as well, for its ghost.
- **Memory:** the two textures are 48 MB at that size, and a byte a texel more (6 MB) is kept for the next ghost. At 5K, with a texel to two pixels each way, 28 MB; at 6K, 39 MB.
- **Shipped:** twenty-five routes, 1.9 MB (the first six pictures' twelve were 942 KB).
- **Not timed in the app:** a 5K or 6K display (a test draws the picture at 5K through the four-read shader). A standalone copy of the shader, timed the same way before this was built, took 0.57 ms of GPU at 5K and 0.79 at 6K; that was without the tooth, which is worked out for every pixel of the display and so costs more there than the tenth of a millisecond it costs here. Nor a GPU slower than an M3 Max, nor the frame on which a texture is first sent.

## Shortcuts

- A texel takes the time of the pass that covers it most, and the finished line's cover. Where a later line overlaps an earlier one's edge by a sliver, that sliver shows with the earlier line (`ponytail:` in `LineRoute.stamp`).
- The stray marks that join separate pieces are part of every unbroken route: 536 cells of them on the Wave.
- The line crosses from one tone's colour to the other's straight through the ground's, so on Paper, Parchment and Blueprint it is the ground's colour for an instant of its two seconds (`ponytail:` in `update`).
- The tracer is not an artist: shading in small pale areas turns into boxy meanders, hatching never follows a form, and at the standard width lettering is illegible.

## What is wrong, and next

- **Filling the screen cuts into three subjects** on a 14-inch screen, and further on a wider display: the Rhinoceros's back and feet, the flea's back and feet, and the Flying Machine's caption ([Filling the screen](#filling-the-screen)). Only the Airship, which lost both ends of its hull, is kept whole (`Etching.whole`). A picture could carry how much of itself may be cut, or be baked to a wider crop.
- **Seen on a desktop by Dave alone.** What the builders judged is all from the render test's pictures. He has since run it on his own desktop and called it great (2026-10-12). No builder has opened the Settings page: the test that does it puts a window on the screen.
- **Nobody has seen the surfaces on a real display** (2026-10-13). Dave picked them from sheets of the render test's pictures, and the builder judged the tooth, the dark tones and dusk from 1:1 crops of the same. Not seen: any of it on a screen at its own size, a change of Surface or of Screen in motion (the tests check its levels a second in and at the end), Follow Day and Night through a real dusk, and the moment the line crosses, when for part of two seconds it is the ground's colour. Not timed: a 5K or 6K display, where the tooth costs more ([Cost](#cost)).
- **The README's and the website's pictures and words are still of grey powder** until they are republished, which waits on Dave's word: `etching-dark.jpg`, `etching-velocipede.jpg`, `etching-pont-neuf.jpg` and `etching.webp`, the site's card and loop, and the README's paragraph ("on a screen of fine gray powder"). `etching.jpg` has been retaken on Paper, so until then the README's hero is paper beside three pictures of powder, and the card cut from it no longer matches the loop's first frame ([Checking it](#checking-it)). The loop's line in `site/media.sh` asks for `etching.screen=1`, which is now Paper's dark tone.
- **GPU is 0.30 to 0.36 ms a frame** on a 14-inch screen, against the 0.35 the lead set for the tooth: two of 24 runs were at or over it.
- **On the dark tones a line drawn once is nearer a line gone over** than on the light ones ([The surfaces](#the-surfaces)). A share other than 62% for those tones would part them.
- **At 1× the picture still stands still for a few seconds at a time** while the stylus goes back along its line: up to 4.8 s on the Wave and 6.5 on the flea, 11 to 14% of the clock. A frame with dials would show them turning then.
- **The Settings page's live copy shows the same moment as the desktop,** so in a picture's first minutes it is a nearly bare screen.
- **A frame with dials** ("frame later"): the screen is 1.40 : 1 on the toy, so a frame costs picture (62% of the display in the mock-ups). Dials would turn about five times to cross the screen; a fine knurl strobes at 15 frames a second, so theirs would be coarse or blurred.
- **More pictures, to research:** Dave, 2026-10-12, having run it on his desktop: "we will likely want to add more etching drawings as options in the future, make a future research note for that". The set is thirteen. For the round that adds to it:
  - **Start with** what the first shortlist of 25 left untried: Ramelli's book wheel, Nolli's plan of Rome, Waldseemüller's world map, Haeckel's line plates of radiolaria. Then more of the kinds already in the set: woodcuts and engravings with a bold line, patent drawings, scientific plates, pattern books, old city plans.
  - **Where the thirteen came from**, each with terms that allow shipping: The Met's Open Access (CC0), the Wellcome Collection (CC BY 4.0, credited), the Getty Research Institute by way of the Internet Archive, the Leventhal Map & Education Center at the Boston Public Library, and US patent drawings ([The pictures](#the-pictures) has every address).
  - **Rules:** nothing religious (classical myth is fine); public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA only, the licence read at its source and quoted in the table; landscape, or a landscape crop.
  - **What to check before a picture goes in:** that it reads at the standard line and not only the fine one (Pont Neuf ships fine only); how long it takes at speed 1 (the set runs 23 to 89 minutes); the longest the screen stands still while the stylus goes back; and what Fill the screen cuts from it on a 14-inch and a wider display ([Filling the screen](#filling-the-screen)).
  - **What one costs:** two routes of about 75 KB each in the app, a line in `etching-credits.tsv`, an entry in `PICTURES` in `bake.py` and a row in the table. The Picture menu stores its pick by place, so new ones go at the end of `Etching.pictures`.
  - The menu is fourteen long already (Random and thirteen) and flat: a `.choice` menu has no groups. A much longer set may want them, by kind or by maker (the lead's view; Dave has not been asked).
